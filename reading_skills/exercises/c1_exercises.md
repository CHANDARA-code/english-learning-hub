## Part 2 — Essay-Level Intellectual Reading

### Full Passage: The Rhetoric of Meritocracy and the Myth of Individual Agency

> **¶1** In contemporary socio-political discourse, the doctrine of meritocracy reigns as an unassailable moral ideal. It posits a social architecture wherein individual talent, industrious effort, and moral fortitude determine socioeconomic ascent, unencumbered by the archaic privileges of birth, caste, or nepotistic patronage. Under this seductive rubric, societal outcomes are deemed intrinsically just: the affluent have legitimately earned their affluence through competitive excellence, while the destitute bear the moral responsibility for their own stagnation.
>
> **¶2** However, critical sociologists argue that this meritocratic rhetoric conceals a profound ideological sleight of hand. By celebrating high-status individuals as the sole architects of their triumph, meritocracy systematically obscures the vast subterranean scaffolding of inherited advantage—cultural capital, elite social networks, zip-code lottery, and generational wealth. The playing field is notoriously uneven long before the competitive race commences. Consequently, rather than dissolving entrenched class hierarchies, meritocracy serves primarily to legitimize them, sanctifying systemic inequality under the veneer of moral desert.
>
> **¶3** Furthermore, this ethos inflicts severe psychological damage across the social spectrum. For the disenfranchised, socioeconomic failure is transformed from a tragic consequence of structural disadvantage into an acute, stigmatizing personal failure of character. Conversely, for the affluent, meritocratic doctrine fosters an insufferable hubris. Convinced that their prosperity is the direct dividend of their superior virtue, elites view the less fortunate not with empathetic solidarity, but with patronizing condescension.
>
> **¶4** Thus, far from functioning as an egalitarian engine of social mobility, unrestrained meritocracy has engendered a deeply fractured civic landscape. To reconstruct a genuinely humane social contract, society must abandon the fantasy that human dignity can be measured through competitive market utility, recognizing instead that communal well-being rests upon mutual obligation rather than triumphant self-congratulation.

## ✅ Answer Key with Analytical Walkthroughs

### Part 1 Solutions

#### Exercise 1: Syntactic Parsing
1. **Grammatical Subject:** `the landmark climate statute`
2. **Main Verb:** `represents` (Object: `an unprecedented constitutional entrenchment of generational equity`)
3. **Concession:** The statute was *"substantially watered down in committee"* (diluted / weakened during legislative negotiations).
4. **Plain English Meaning:** Despite being compromised by lobbyists, the law represents a historic legal breakthrough for protecting the rights of future generations.

#### Exercise 2: Unstated Presuppositions
1. **Answer: B.** The phrase *"at last ceased its transparently hollow platitudes"* presupposes that the administration was repeatedly uttering fake, empty words for a very long time until now.
2. **Answer: A.** Saying the second novel failed to replicate the *"luminous stylistic audacity that distinguished her debut"* presupposes that her debut was distinguished by daring, brilliant style.

#### Exercise 3: Rhetorical Subtext
1. **Answer: B.** Describing a $40 billion failure as "mildly overoptimistic" is devastating ironic understatement (litotes). The author means the forecasts were totally disastrous.
2. **Answer: C.** *"Unbridled skepticism"* means complete, unrestricted doubt/disbelief. Advocates had zero trust in the pledge.

#### Exercise 4: Distractor Deconstruction
- **Option A:** Invalid because of the **Causation Fallacy**; the text never links burial goods to Neanderthal extinction.
- **Option B:** Invalid because of the **Scope Drift Trap**; it generalizes "scarcity of surviving artifacts" to an absolute claim that "all Neanderthal communities" practiced rituals.
- **Option C:** Invalid because of the **Unsupported Extrapolation Trap**; the text never claims Neanderthal culture was "superior" to modern human culture.

---

### Part 2 Solutions

#### Exercise 5: Dialectic Mapping
1. **Thesis (¶1):** Meritocracy is presented as a just system where individual talent and hard work dictate success.
2. **Antithesis (¶2):** Meritocracy is a myth that masks inherited socioeconomic privileges and legitimizes systemic inequality.
3. **Psychological Costs (¶3):** Produces shame and self-blame among the poor; breeds arrogance and contempt among elites.
4. **Synthesis (¶4):** Call to abandon market-driven meritocracy in favor of mutual obligation and human dignity.

#### Exercise 6: CAE Multiple Choice
1. **Answer: B.** The author uses "unassailable" with critical irony to show that society treats meritocracy as untouchable even though it hides deep structural injustice.
2. **Answer: B.** *"Sleight of hand"* (ល្បិចបន្លំភ្នែក) metaphorically denotes a clever deception that tricks observers into looking at individual effort while hiding generational privilege.
3. **Answer: B.** Evidence (¶3): *"fosters an insufferable hubris... view the less fortunate not with empathetic solidarity, but with patronizing condescension."*

#### Exercise 7: Yes / No / Not Given
1. **NO.** The author explicitly argues that inherited privilege (*generational wealth, social networks*) heavily dictates success.
2. **YES.** Evidence (¶3): failure is transformed into *"an acute, stigmatizing personal failure of character."*
3. **NOT GIVEN.** The text makes no mention of Northern European countries or specific geographical jurisdictions.
4. **NO.** Evidence (¶4): Author demands society *"abandon the fantasy that human dignity can be measured through competitive market utility"*.

#### Exercise 8: Cohesive Restoration
- **Answer: C (At the conclusion of Paragraph 3).** Paragraph 3 analyzes how elite hubris destroys empathy. Adding *"When winners believe their success is purely self-made, the bonds of social cohesion fray irreparably"* perfectly concludes this psychological analysis and creates an ideal bridge into Paragraph 4's opening (*"Thus, far from functioning as an egalitarian engine..."*).
