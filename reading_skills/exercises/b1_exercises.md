## Part 2 — Essay-Level Reading Skills

### Full Passage: The E-Bike Revolution in Modern Towns

> **¶1** In cities around the world, electric bicycles—commonly known as e-bikes—are rapidly transforming personal transportation. Equipped with a small electric motor that assists the rider when pedaling, e-bikes allow individuals to travel across hilly terrain and longer distances without arriving at their destination drenched in sweat. As urban traffic congestion reaches record highs, commuters are increasingly choosing two wheels over four.
>
> **¶2** The environmental and financial arguments favoring e-bikes are compelling. Compared to petrol-powered automobiles, electric bicycles consume a fraction of the energy and produce zero direct greenhouse emissions. Charging an e-bike battery costs mere cents per day, offering colossal savings compared to monthly public transit passes or car insurance. For urban dwellers battling the rising cost of living, swapping a second family car for an e-bike is an easy economic decision.
>
> **¶3** However, the widespread adoption of e-bikes introduces genuine urban planning challenges. Because e-bikes travel considerably faster than traditional bicycles—often reaching speeds of 25 to 32 kilometers per hour—conflicts on shared pedestrian sidewalks and narrow bicycle paths have multiplied. City planners must construct dedicated, widened lanes separated from both fast-moving cars and vulnerable pedestrians to ensure communal safety.
>
> **¶4** In summary, while regulatory and infrastructure hurdles remain, the momentum behind electric cycling is undeniable. With thoughtful investment in bike highways and sensible traffic laws, cities can unlock cleaner air and healthier citizens through the e-bike revolution.

## ✅ Answer Key with Explanations

### Part 1 Solutions

#### Exercise 1: Topic Sentences
1. **Answer: B.** Sentence B provides the overarching general claim (*long-term cognitive and emotional benefits*), while A is a specific detail and C is an irrelevant sub-point.
2. **Answer: B.** Sentence B states the broad crisis and call to action. A is a supporting reason and C is a specific historical example.
3. **Answer: C.** Sentence C balances the advantages and drawbacks. A and B are one-sided supporting points.

#### Exercise 2: Pronoun Tracking
1. **Answer: B (Electric cars).** The clause states *"Because they rely on rechargeable lithium batteries..."*; cars use batteries, not tailpipe emissions or cities.
2. **Answer: C (Doubling parking fees in the center).** *"This decision"* refers to the action taken by the council in the preceding sentence (*"voted to double parking fees"*).
3. **Answer: A (Reading novels in English).** *"The former"* refers to the first of two mentioned items (reading novels); *"the latter"* refers to the second (studying grammar rules).

#### Exercise 3: Guessing Meaning
1. **Answer: B (Careful and economical with money).** Clue: *"Unlike her sister who was completely frugal... Jessica spent her entire paycheck on expensive clothes."* The contrast shows the sister did the opposite of spending recklessly. (ន័យខ្មែរ: សន្សំសំចៃ).
2. **Answer: A (Broken down and in poor condition).** Clue: Landlord warned tenants not to use it until repairs were completed. (ន័យខ្មែរ: ទ្រុឌទ្រោម/ចាស់បាក់បែក).
3. **Answer: B (Active primarily during the night).** Clue: They sleep during daytime and hunt when night falls. (ន័យខ្មែរ: សត្វរាត្រីចរ/រកស៊ីពេលយប់).

#### Exercise 4: Irrelevant Sentence
- **Answer: C.** The entire paragraph focuses on cardiovascular health and exercise benefits. Sentence C wanders off into teenage fashion and branded running shoe status symbols.

#### Exercise 7: True / False / Doesn't Say
1. **TRUE.** Evidence (¶1): *"motor that assists the rider when pedaling"* — requires pedaling.
2. **DOESN'T SAY.** The text states charging costs mere cents per day, but mentions **nothing** about the number of hours required to charge.
3. **TRUE.** Evidence (¶2): *"consume a fraction of the energy and produce zero direct greenhouse emissions."*
4. **FALSE.** Evidence (¶3 & ¶4): Planners *must* construct them, and infrastructure hurdles *remain*; they have not already completed them.

#### Exercise 8: Sentence Insertion
- **Answer: B.** The sentence adds another economic benefit (*parking fees and fuel permits*), making it a perfect logical fit directly following the financial savings on insurance and transit passes in Paragraph 2.
