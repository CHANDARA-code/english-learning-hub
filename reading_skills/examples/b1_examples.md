## Mock Exam Passage: The Rise of Urban Community Gardens

**Passage Word Count:** 302 words  
**Recommended Reading Time:** 2 minutes 30 seconds  
**Total Task Time:** 6 minutes  

> **¶1** In recent years, community gardens have become increasingly popular in large cities across the world. In crowded urban neighborhoods where most residents live in small apartments without private yards, these shared green spaces offer people a unique opportunity to connect with nature. Abandoned parking lots, neglected rooftops, and empty parcels of land are being transformed into productive plots where locals grow fresh vegetables, herbs, and flowers.
>
> **¶2** There are several clear benefits associated with these community projects. First, they provide city dwellers with access to fresh, healthy produce at a very low cost. Because organic food in supermarkets is often expensive, growing their own tomatoes, spinach, and beans helps families save money on their weekly grocery bills. In addition, working in the soil offers significant physical and mental health advantages. Doctors have noted that gardening reduces daily stress, lowers blood pressure, and encourages people to spend time outdoors in the fresh air.
>
> **¶3** Furthermore, community gardens play a crucial role in strengthening neighborhood relationships. In modern cities, people often do not even know the names of the individuals living next door. However, community gardens act as welcoming social hubs where neighbors of different ages, backgrounds, and professions can meet and collaborate. Experienced older gardeners frequently teach younger members how to plant seeds, care for seedlings, and produce rich compost.
>
> **¶4** Despite these evident benefits, maintaining an urban garden is not without its difficulties. Organizers frequently struggle to secure long-term access to land, as property developers often wish to purchase the space to construct commercial buildings. Moreover, volunteers must dedicate several hours every week to water plants, remove weeds, and repair wooden fences. Nevertheless, the majority of urban gardeners agree that the rewards of fresh food and new friendships are well worth the effort.

## Step-by-Step Question Walkthrough & Answer Key

### Multiple Choice Solutions

**1. Answer: B**
- **Evidence in text (¶1):** *"In crowded urban neighborhoods where most residents live in small apartments without private yards, these shared green spaces offer people a unique opportunity to connect with nature."*
- **Paraphrase Analysis:** *"without private yards"* = *people without gardens*; *"connect with nature"* = *be close to nature*.
- **Distractor Traps:** A is irrelevant; C uses extreme word *"all"*; D distorts *"abandoned parking lots"* into *"parking spaces"*.

**2. Answer: C**
- **Evidence in text (¶2):** *"Doctors have noted that gardening reduces daily stress, lowers blood pressure..."*
- **Paraphrase Analysis:** *"reduces daily stress"* = *relieves mental tension*.
- **Distractor Traps:** A uses extreme claim *"cures most serious conditions"*; B is not supported; D is not mentioned in ¶2.

**3. Answer: C**
- **Evidence in text (¶3):** *"In modern cities, people often do not even know the names of the individuals living next door."*
- **Paraphrase Analysis:** *"do not even know the names"* = *frequently lack connection with those who live near them*.
- **Distractor Traps:** A contradicts the text; B contradicts text (*"frequently teach younger members"*); D is an unmentioned assumption.

**4. Answer: B**
- **Evidence in text (¶4):** *"Organizers frequently struggle to secure long-term access to land, as property developers often wish to purchase the space to construct commercial buildings."*
- **Paraphrase Analysis:** *"construct commercial buildings"* = *build on the land*.
- **Distractor Traps:** A and D are never mentioned; C contradicts the collaborative nature described in the passage.

### True / False / Doesn't Say Solutions

**5. Answer: FALSE**
- **Evidence in text (¶2):** *"Because organic food in supermarkets is often expensive, growing their own tomatoes... helps families save money..."*
- **Reason:** The statement says supermarket food is *cheaper*, which directly contradicts the text.

**6. Answer: TRUE**
- **Evidence in text (¶3):** *"Experienced older gardeners frequently teach younger members how to plant seeds..."*
- **Reason:** This is a direct paraphrase of sharing gardening knowledge.

**7. Answer: DOESN'T SAY**
- **Evidence in text:** The passage mentions that organizers struggle to secure land and need volunteers, but mentions **nothing** about government financial grants or compost subsidies.
- **Rule:** Do not assume government assistance exists; the passage doesn't mention it.

**8. Answer: TRUE**
- **Evidence in text (¶4):** *"Nevertheless, the majority of urban gardeners agree that the rewards of fresh food and new friendships are well worth the effort."*
- **Reason:** *"well worth the effort"* directly confirms *a worthwhile experience*.
