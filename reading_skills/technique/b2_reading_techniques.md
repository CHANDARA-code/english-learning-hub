# 🔴 B2 Reading Techniques — Upper Intermediate
## បច្ចេកទេសអាន កម្រិត B2 (មធ្យមកម្រិតខ្ពស់)

> **Goal at B2:** Read with a high degree of independence on a wide range of general, professional, and academic topics. Understand articles, reports, and essays where writers adopt particular stances or viewpoints. Understand detailed arguments, identify underlying attitudes, and synthesize information across paragraphs.
> **Typical Exams & Benchmarks:** Cambridge B2 First (FCE), IELTS Band 5.5–6.5, TOEFL iBT 72–94, CEFR B2.
> **Target Reading Speed:** 200–240 words per minute (wpm).

## 1. From B1 to B2: What Changes

| Area | B1 Reading | B2 Reading | ន័យខ្មែរ (ការផ្លាស់ប្តូរ) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | Direct facts & explicit points | Line of argument, implications, and nuances | ពីការស្វែងរកការពិតផ្ទាល់ ទៅជាការតាមដានទឡ្ហីករណ៍ និងអត្ថន័យបង្កប់ |

## 2. Text Architecture & Macro-Structure

B2 texts follow recognizable structural blueprints. Identifying the blueprint within the first 60 seconds helps you predict where answers are located.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               COMMON B2 TEXT ARCHITECTURES             │
├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Problem → Solution    │ Problem stated → Causes →   │
│                          │ Proposed interventions → Evaluation │
├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Discursive / Balanced │ Issue → Arguments For →     │
│                          │ Arguments Against → Synthesis/Stance│
├──────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Chronological / Case  │ Historical origin → Current │
│                          │ status → Future implications│
└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┘
```

### The "PEEL" Reverse-Engine Method
When reading a body paragraph, expect writers to use:
- **P (Point):** First 1–2 sentences present the claim.
- **E (Explanation):** Next 1–2 sentences unpack why/how.
- **E (Evidence/Data):** Specific study, case example, quotation, or statistic.
- **L (Link):** Final sentence summarizes or transitions to the next idea.

## 3. Advanced Paraphrase Recognition

In B2 exams (such as IELTS Reading and Cambridge FCE), question prompts **never** use the identical phrasing found in the passage. They manipulate language in three distinct ways:

### A. Nominalization (Transforming Verbs into Nouns)
- *Passage:* "Because the polar ice caps are melting rapidly, sea levels will rise."
- *Question:* "What consequence is caused by the **rapid liquefaction of glacial ice**?"

### B. Antonym with Negation
- *Passage:* "The committee found the proposal financially unfeasible."
- *Question:* "The plan was rejected because it **was not deemed economically viable**."

### C. Collocation and Phrasal Shift
- *Passage:* "The company managed to **weather the economic crisis** without firing staff."
- *Question:* "The enterprise **survived financial turmoil** while maintaining full employment."

## 4. Tracking Argument Flow & Discourse Markers

Discourse markers act as road signs guiding the reader through twists in the author's logic:

| Function | Markers | What You Must Realize as a Reader |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Concession (សារភាព/ទទួលស្គាល់)** | *Admittedly, granted, even though, despite the fact that* | The author temporarily acknowledges a counter-point before destroying it. |
| **Adversative Pivot (បត់គំនិត)** | *However, nevertheless, nonetheless, yet, on the flip side* | **Warning:** The real point usually comes **after** this word! |
| **Causal Consequence (លទ្ធផល)** | *Consequently, as a corollary, thereby, hence, thus* | Preceding sentence is the cause; following clause is the outcome tested. |
| **Exemplification & Clarification** | *Namely, notably, specifically, that is to say* | Detailed clarification of an abstract concept. |

> 💡 **Golden Rule:** When an IELTS or FCE question asks about the author's ultimate conclusion, look for sentences starting with *Nevertheless, In the final analysis,* or *Overall*.

## 5. Inferencing: Reading Between the Lines

An **inference** (ការទាញសេចក្តីសន្និដ្ឋាន) is a logical conclusion derived from evidence rather than an explicit statement.

### The Inference Formula
$$\text{Passage Evidence} + \text{Objective Logic} = \text{Valid Inference}$$

#### Example Walkthrough:
> *Text:* "Although the new electric vehicle was priced at half the cost of its European competitors, showrooms remained largely deserted throughout the winter months, with dealerships citing consumers' chronic anxiety regarding the country's sparse charging network."
>
> - **Fact 1:** The vehicle is cheap.
> - **Fact 2:** Few people bought or inspected the vehicle.
> - **Fact 3:** Customers were anxious about limited charging stations.
> - **Valid Inference:** Consumer reluctance was driven by inadequate infrastructure rather than vehicle affordability.
> - **Invalid Distortion:** The vehicle had mechanical defects during cold winter weather (Unsupported assumption).

## 6. Decoding Author Tone, Stance & Hedging

Academic and professional writers rarely make absolute claims (*"All children become violent from video games"*). Instead, they use **Hedging** (ភាសាប្រុងប្រយ័ត្ន).

### Recognizing Hedging Devices
- **Modal Verbs of Probability:** *may, might, could, would appear to*
- **Cautious Verbs:** *suggests, implies, indicates, tends to, purports to*
- **Adverbs of Degree:** *arguably, predominantly, ostensibly, largely, relatively*
- **Concessive Clauses:** *While preliminary findings seem promising, caution is warranted.*

### Stance Spectrum
```
Strongly Negative ──> Skeptical ──> Objective/Neutral ──> Cautiously Optimistic ──> Vehemently Positive
     [-2]                [-1]              [0]                    [+1]                     [+2]
```
Pay close attention to evaluative adjectives: *dubious, speculative, groundbreaking, flawed, compelling*.

## 7. Mastering B2 / IELTS / FCE Question Formats

### 1. Heading Matching (Matching Headings to Paragraphs)
- **Problem:** Many students read only the first line of the paragraph. If the paragraph opens with a background sentence or counter-argument, you will pick the wrong heading.
- **Solution:** 
  1. Read the headings list first, crossing out already-used headings.
  2. Underline distinguishing keywords in headings (*causes vs effects vs solutions*).
  3. Skim the **entire** paragraph to capture the collective thrust, not just one isolated sentence.

### 2. True / False / Not Given (and Yes / No / Not Given)
Use this strict 3-way decision matrix:

```
Does the passage directly agree with the statement?
 ├─ YES (Same fact & meaning) ──────────> TRUE / YES
 ├─ NO (Directly contradicts / opposite) ─> FALSE / NO
 └─ CANNOT BE PROVED from the text ───────> NOT GIVEN
```
> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** Do not confuse *False* with *Not Given*. If a statement says *"Solar power is cheaper than wind power"*, and the passage only says *"Solar power has become very affordable"*, the answer is **NOT GIVEN** (no direct comparison exists).

### 3. Summary Completion with Word Bank
- Identify the grammatical part of speech required for each gap (Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb).
- Predict collocations and semantic categories (*e.g., must be a negative adjective describing weather*).
- Scan the text for the section matching the summary's chronology.

## 8. Elimination Tactics for Distractors

In multiple-choice questions, test creators intentionally design 3 incorrect options (Distractors). Eliminate them using the **F.A.T.E.** rubric:

- **F — False Information:** Directly contradicts a detail in the passage.
- **A — Absolute Generalization:** Uses words like *exclusively, entirely, universally, inevitable* when the text was hedged.
- **T — True, but Irrelevant:** True according to the passage, but does not actually answer the specific question asked.
- **E — Extrapolated / Not Given:** Goes beyond the evidence provided in the text.

## 9. Exam Time Management & Pacing

For standard B2 examinations (e.g., IELTS Academic Reading: 3 texts, 40 questions, 60 minutes):

| Phase | Duration | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Passage 1** | 16–17 minutes | Factual/Descriptive text; maximize accuracy here |
| **Passage 2** | 18–20 minutes | Discursive/Analytical text; maintain rhythm |
| **Passage 3** | 22–24 minutes | Complex conceptual/argumentative text |
| **Buffer / Checking** | 2–3 minutes | Ensure no blanks remain on answer sheet |

## 10. B2 Self-Check List

- [ ] Can I distinguish between an author's personal viewpoint and an opinion they are merely quoting?
- [ ] Do I immediately recognize grammatical nominalization when scanning for question keywords?
- [ ] Can I identify when a discourse connector (*However, Yet*) marks a decisive shift in the central argument?
- [ ] Am I avoiding the trap of choosing True/False based on external real-world knowledge?
- [ ] Can I read a 350-word academic paragraph and summarize its core function in a single sentence?
