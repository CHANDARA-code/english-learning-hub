# 🟡 B1 Reading Techniques — Intermediate
## បច្ចេកទេសអាន កម្រិត B1 (មធ្យម)

> **Goal at B1:** Understand the main points of clear standard texts on familiar matters (work, school, leisure, travel, daily life). Locate specific predictable information in everyday material such as brochures, short articles, personal letters, and news stories.
> **Typical Exams & Benchmarks:** Cambridge B1 Preliminary (PET), IELTS Band 4.5–5.0, TOEFL iBT 42–71, CEFR B1.
> **Target Reading Speed:** 140–180 words per minute (wpm) for general comprehension.

## 1. What B1 Reading Looks Like

At B1, you transition from reading short phrases to understanding multi-paragraph articles and structured prose.

| Area | B1 Reader Target | ន័យខ្មែរ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Idea (Gist)** | Identify the central point of each paragraph | កំណត់គំនិតចម្បងនៃកថាខណ្ឌនីមួយៗ |
| **Factual Details** | Find names, dates, quantities, places, and facts quickly | ស្វែងរកព័ត៌មានជាក់លាក់ (កាលបរិច្ឆេទ ឈ្មោះ តួលេខ) |
| **Vocabulary Range** | ~2,000–2,500 common active words; tolerate unfamiliar words | ពាក្យទូទៅប្រមាណ ២០០០–២៥០០ ពាក្យ |
| **Sentence Structure** | Compound and basic complex sentences (*because, although, if, which*) | ប្រយោគផ្សំ និងប្រយោគស្មុគស្មាញកម្រិតមូលដ្ឋាន |
| **Text Length** | Texts of 250–450 words (exam passages) | អត្ថបទប្រវែង ២៥០ ដល់ ៤៥០ ពាក្យ |

## 2. The Core Distinction: Skimming vs Scanning

Many learners fail exams because they read every word from start to finish at the same slow pace. You must switch gears depending on the question.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    READING TOOLKIT                           │
├──────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┤
│ 🚀 SKIMMING (អានត្រួសៗ)      │ 🔍 SCANNING (អានរាវរក)        │
│ Purpose: Find the GIST/topic │ Purpose: Find a SPECIFIC FACT │
│ Speed: Very fast (300+ wpm)  │ Speed: Darting eyes           │
│ What to read: Titles, first  │ What to look for: Numbers,    │
│ & last lines, main nouns     │ Capital letters, keywords     │
└──────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘
```

---

## 3. Skimming for Gist (អានត្រួសៗរកគំនិតចម្បង)

**Gist** means the overall meaning of a text. Do **not** stop for difficult vocabulary when skimming.

### The 30-Second Skimming Protocol
1. **Title & Subheadings:** Predict what the text is about.
2. **First Paragraph (Introduction):** Read the whole paragraph to catch the background and main topic.
3. **First Sentence of Each Body Paragraph:** This is usually the **Topic Sentence** (ប្រយោគប្រធានបទ) containing the paragraph's key point.
4. **Final Sentence of the Conclusion:** Summarizes the writer's final thought.

#### Example Skim
> *(First line)* Many people dream of opening their own coffee shop, but running a small café is far harder than most imagine. *(Middle lines skipped...)* *(Last line)* Consequently, only those with strong business discipline tend to survive their first year.
> 
> 👉 **Instant Gist:** Running a café is difficult and requires strict business discipline, contrary to popular belief.

## 4. Scanning for Specific Information (អានរករឿងជាក់លាក់)

Scanning is looking for **one specific piece of data** without reading the surrounding sentences.

### How to Scan Effectively
1. **Identify the "Anchor Keyword":**
   - Dates / Numbers: *1998, 25%, $500, three days*
   - Proper Nouns (Capital letters): *London, Professor Miller, UNESCO*
   - Technical terms or unique nouns: *photosynthesis, turbine, telescope*
2. **Move Your Eyes in a 'Z' or 'S' Pattern:** Scan backwards or diagonally so your brain doesn't get pulled into reading the full sentences.
3. **Stop & Read Narrowly:** Once your eyes hit the anchor keyword, read **one sentence before and one sentence after** to confirm the answer.

## 5. Guessing Unknown Words from Context (ទស្សន៍ទាយន័យពាក្យ)

Do not panic when you encounter an unknown word. Use these 4 context clues:

| Clue Type | How It Works | Example Sentence & Analysis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Synonym / Restatement** | The writer explains the word nearby using *or, in other words, that is* | *The town was **tranquil**, in other words, extremely peaceful and quiet.* → tranquil = peaceful |
| **Contrast Clue** | Signal words (*but, however, unlike, whereas, while*) show the opposite | *Unlike his **gregarious** brother who loves large parties, Mark is shy and quiet.* → gregarious = outgoing/social |
| **Cause & Effect** | Cause-and-effect connectors explain the situation | *Because the bridge was **frail**, heavy trucks were forbidden from crossing.* → frail = weak/fragile |
| **Example Clue** | Examples (*such as, for instance, including*) clarify the category | *Pachyderms, such as elephants, rhinos, and hippos, have thick skin.* → pachyderm = thick-skinned large mammal |

## 6. Tracking Reference Words & Pronouns (ការតាមដានពាក្យជំនួស)

Exam writers frequently test whether you know what pronouns refer to.

```
Sentence 1: Solar panels generate electricity directly from sunlight.
Sentence 2: Although THEY are expensive to install, THIS TECHNOLOGY reduces energy bills.
           └── "THEY" = Solar panels             └── "THIS TECHNOLOGY" = Solar power generation
```

### Common B1 Reference Words
- **Personal Pronouns:** *it, they, them, he, she*
- **Demonstratives:** *this, that, these, those* (often combined with a noun: *this problem, that approach*)
- **Relative Pronouns:** *which, who, where, whose*
- **Substitutes:** *the former (អាខាងដើម), the latter (អាខាងចុង), one / ones*

## 7. Deconstructing B1 Sentences (ការបំបែកប្រយោគ)

When a sentence feels too long, strip it down to its **Core Kernel**: `[Subject] + [Verb] + [Object/Complement]`.

### Deconstruction Example
> *Although many students in secondary schools find mathematics challenging, **regular practice using interactive digital applications consistently improves their test scores**.*

- **Subordinate / Context Clause:** *Although many students... challenging* (Shows contrast)
- **Core Subject:** *regular practice*
- **Subject Modifier:** *using interactive digital applications*
- **Core Verb:** *improves*
- **Core Object:** *their test scores*
- **Takeaway:** Practice boosts test scores.

## 8. Mastering B1 Exam Question Types

### Question Type 1: Multiple Choice (A, B, C, D)
- **Strategy:** Read the question stem first. Do **not** read options A–D immediately, as distractors will confuse you. Formulate your own answer from the text, then find the option that matches your idea.
- **Trap:** Option repeats 3 exact words from the passage, but changes the meaning (*Word-Match Distractor*).

### Question Type 2: True / False / Doesn't Say (Not Given)
- **TRUE:** The passage clearly states the exact same fact (using synonyms).
- **FALSE:** The passage directly contradicts the statement.
- **DOESN'T SAY (NOT GIVEN):** The passage mentions the topic, but does not provide enough information to confirm or deny the statement.
- **Rule:** Never guess using your personal general knowledge! Rely strictly on what is printed on the page.

### Question Type 3: Matching Notices & Short Texts
- Read the short notice (signs, warnings, emails, menus).
- Ask: **Who wrote this? For whom? What is the main action required?**
- Look for modal verbs of obligation: *must, have to, permitted, forbidden, not allowed*.

## 9. Common B1 Reading Traps & Pitfalls

| Trap Name | How It Works | How to Defeat It |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Word-Spotting Trap** | A wrong option copies an exact word from the passage | Always look for **synonyms** instead of identical words. Correct answers almost always paraphrase. |
| **The Absolute Word Trap** | Options with *always, all, never, impossible, only, every* | The text usually uses cautious words (*often, mostly, rarely, usually*). Eliminate extreme options unless text explicitly confirms. |
| **The Outside Knowledge Trap** | You know a fact is true in real life, but the text says otherwise | Stick **strictly** to what the passage says. If the text does not say it, it is *Not Given*. |
| **Over-Reading** | Spending 4 minutes reading a sentence you don't understand | Move on! You can answer 80% of questions without understanding every single sentence. |

## 10. The 5-Step B1 Reading Routine & Checklist

```
Step 1: 30-Second Skim    ──> Read title, first line, last line (Get the big picture)
Step 2: Question Check    ──> Underline anchor keywords in the questions
Step 3: Targeted Scan     ──> Locate the relevant paragraph without reading line-by-line
Step 4: Micro-Read        ──> Read the 2 sentences around the keyword with total focus
Step 5: Paraphrase Match  ──> Match the idea (not just identical words) to the answer
```

### B1 Self-Check Checklist
- [ ] Can I identify the topic sentence of a paragraph in under 10 seconds?
- [ ] Do I resist the urge to look up every single unknown word in a dictionary?
- [ ] Can I distinguish between an exact word trap and an accurate synonym paraphrase?
- [ ] Can I identify who or what *it, this,* or *they* refers to in a complex sentence?
- [ ] Am I finishing 300-word reading passages within 4–5 minutes including questions?
