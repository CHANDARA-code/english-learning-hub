# 🔴 B2 Writing Techniques — Upper Intermediate
## បច្ចេកទេសសរសេរ កម្រិត B2 (មធ្យមកម្រិតខ្ពស់)

> **Goal at B2:** Write clear, detailed text on a wide range of topics. Develop an argument, weigh advantages and disadvantages, and support points with relevant examples.
> **Typical tasks:** Discursive/opinion essays, reports, reviews, formal letters and emails, articles (140–260 words; IELTS Task 2: 250+).

## 1. From B1 to B2: What Changes

| Area | B1 | B2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ideas | Simple opinion + reason | Argument with **both sides** and a justified conclusion |
| Paragraphs | Point + example | Point + **explanation** + evidence + **link back** |
| Linking | *and, but, so, because* | *whereas, despite, consequently, therefore, on the contrary* |
| Grammar | Basic tenses, 1st conditional | Passive, 2nd/3rd conditionals, participle clauses, modals of deduction |
| Vocabulary | Everyday words | Topic vocabulary + collocations (*raise awareness, pose a threat*) |
| Register | Mostly neutral | Can switch between **formal** and **informal** |

## 2. The Developed Paragraph (PEEL)

**PEEL** = **P**oint · **E**xplain · **E**vidence/Example · **L**ink

| Step | Purpose | Sentence starters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **P**oint (ចំណុច) | State the main idea | *One major advantage of… is that…* |
| **E**xplain (ពន្យល់) | Say *why* or *how* | *This is because… / This means that…* |
| **E**vidence (ភស្តុតាង) | Example, statistic, experience | *For instance,… / A recent survey found that…* |
| **L**ink (ភ្ជាប់) | Connect back to the question/thesis | *Therefore,… / This clearly shows that…* |

### Example
> **(P)** One major benefit of public transport is that it reduces traffic congestion in large cities. **(E)** When more people travel by bus or train, fewer private cars are on the road, which allows traffic to move more freely. **(E)** In Singapore, for example, an efficient metro system combined with high car taxes has kept rush-hour delays far lower than in many other Asian capitals. **(L)** Therefore, investing in public transport is one of the most effective ways for governments to tackle urban traffic problems.

## 3. Writing a Strong Thesis Statement

The thesis statement (សេចក្តីថ្លែងគោលជំហរ) is the **last sentence of your introduction**. It tells the reader your position and what your essay will cover.

| ❌ Weak | ✅ Strong |
| :--- | :--- |
| *This essay is about social media.* | *Although social media helps people stay connected, I believe its negative effects on young people's mental health outweigh its benefits.* |
| *There are good and bad points.* | *While working from home saves time and money, it can also lead to isolation and a poor work–life balance.* |

### Thesis formula
> **Although / While** [other side], **I believe / I would argue that** [your position] **because** [reason 1] **and** [reason 2].

### Introduction structure (3 steps)
1. **Hook / background** — a general statement about the topic.
2. **Paraphrase the question** — show you understand the issue.
3. **Thesis** — your position.

## 4. Essay Types and Structures

### A. Opinion Essay (Agree/Disagree)
| Paragraph | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| Introduction | Background + clear opinion |
| Body 1 | Reason 1 (PEEL) |
| Body 2 | Reason 2 (PEEL) |
| Body 3 *(optional)* | Opposing view + **rebuttal** (why it is weaker) |
| Conclusion | Restate opinion + summarise reasons |

### B. Discussion Essay (Discuss both views)
| Paragraph | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| Introduction | Present the two views + (your opinion if asked) |
| Body 1 | View A with reasons |
| Body 2 | View B with reasons |
| Conclusion | Summary + your balanced judgement |

### C. Advantages & Disadvantages Essay
| Paragraph | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| Introduction | Topic + outline |
| Body 1 | Advantages |
| Body 2 | Disadvantages |
| Conclusion | Which side is stronger (if asked) |

### D. Problem & Solution Essay
| Paragraph | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| Introduction | Present the issue |
| Body 1 | Problems/causes |
| Body 2 | Solutions (match each solution to a problem) |
| Conclusion | Summary + most effective solution |

## 5. Cohesion: Beyond Basic Linking Words

Cohesion (ភាពស៊ីសង្វាក់) is not only connectors. It also includes **reference**, **substitution**, and **lexical chains**.

### A. Advanced connectors
| Function | Connectors |
| :--- | :--- |
| Contrast | *whereas, while, nevertheless, on the other hand, despite / in spite of + noun/-ing* |
| Concession | *admittedly, it is true that… , but…, even though* |
| Cause/Effect | *consequently, therefore, thus, as a consequence, due to / owing to + noun, leading to* |
| Addition | *furthermore, moreover, what is more, not only… but also…* |
| Example | *to illustrate, a clear example of this is…, namely* |
| Emphasis | *in fact, indeed, particularly, above all* |

⚠️ **Grammar of connectors:**
- *despite / in spite of / due to* + **noun or -ing**: *Despite **the rain**, … / Despite **being** tired, …*
- *although / even though / whereas* + **clause**: *Although **it was raining**, …*

### B. Reference words — avoid repeating nouns
> *Many cities have introduced bike-sharing schemes. **These schemes** are popular because **they** are cheap. **This** has encouraged more people to cycle.*

Use: *this, these, that, those, it, they, such + noun, the former / the latter*.

### C. Synonyms and word families
Instead of repeating *problem* five times: *issue, challenge, difficulty, concern, drawback*.
Instead of *important*: *essential, crucial, significant, vital, key*.

## 6. Sentence Variety and B2 Grammar

| Structure | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Passive voice** (formal, objective) | *Millions of tonnes of plastic **are thrown away** every year.* |
| **Second conditional** (hypothetical) | *If governments **invested** more in schools, fewer students **would drop out**.* |
| **Third conditional** (past regret) | *If I **had known** about the course, I **would have joined** it.* |
| **Participle clause** | ***Having finished** the project, the team celebrated. / **Located** in the city centre, the hotel is convenient.* |
| **Non-defining relative clause** | *Online learning, **which became common during the pandemic**, is still popular.* |
| **Cleft sentence** (emphasis) | ***What worries me most is** the cost. / **It is** the young **who** suffer most.* |
| **Not only… but also** | *It is **not only** cheaper **but also** faster.* |
| **Modals of deduction** | *This **might** lead to… / It **must** have been difficult…* |

### Vary sentence openings
Don't start every sentence with the subject. Try:
- An adverb: ***Surprisingly,** …*
- A prepositional phrase: ***In many developing countries,** …*
- A dependent clause: ***Although** it is expensive, …*
- A participle: ***Faced with** rising costs, many families…*

## 7. Formal vs Informal Register

| Informal | Formal |
| :--- | :--- |
| *a lot of* | *a great deal of / numerous / a significant number of* |
| *get* | *obtain / receive / become* |
| *find out* | *discover / determine* |
| *go up / go down* | *increase / decrease* |
| *think about* | *consider* |
| *need* | *require* |
| *kids* | *children* |
| *really bad* | *extremely harmful / serious* |
| *I think* | *It could be argued that / I would argue that* |
| *can't, don't* | *cannot, do not* |

**Formal writing rules:**
- ❌ No contractions, slang, or exclamation marks.
- ❌ Avoid *you* for general statements → use *people, individuals, one*.
- ✅ Use passive and nominalisation where natural: *The decision was made to…*

## 8. Hedging: Sounding Balanced

Hedging (ការនិយាយដោយប្រុងប្រយ័ត្ន) makes claims less absolute and more academic.

| ❌ Too strong | ✅ Hedged |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Social media causes depression.* | *Excessive use of social media **may contribute to** depression.* |
| *All students hate exams.* | ***Many** students **tend to** find exams stressful.* |
| *This will solve the problem.* | *This **is likely to** reduce the problem.* |

Hedging words: *may, might, could, tend to, seem to, appear to, it is possible that, generally, in most cases, to some extent*.

## 9. Other B2 Genres: Report, Review, Article

### Report
- Title + **headings** (Introduction, Findings, Recommendations)
- Formal, factual, often passive: *It was found that… / It is recommended that…*

### Review
- Engaging title, brief description, **evaluation** (strengths/weaknesses), recommendation.
- Language: *The plot is gripping… / It falls short in… / I would highly recommend it to…*

### Article
- Catchy title, direct questions to the reader, lively tone.
- *Have you ever wondered…? / Let's face it,… / So, what can we do?*

## 10. Common B2 Mistakes

| ❌ Mistake | ✅ Correction | Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Despite it was raining,…* | *Despite the rain,… / Although it was raining,…* | *Despite* + noun |
| *In the other hand,…* | *On the other hand,…* | Fixed phrase |
| *Moreover, but it is also…* | *Moreover, it is…* | Don't double connectors |
| *The number of cars have increased.* | *The number of cars has increased.* | *The number* = singular |
| *It can be argue that…* | *It can be argued that…* | Passive needs past participle |
| *People who lives in cities…* | *People who live in cities…* | Agreement in relative clause |
| *Make a research / do a mistake* | *Do research / make a mistake* | Collocation |
| *Nowadays, technology is very important in our life.* (cliché) | *Technology now shapes almost every aspect of daily life.* | Avoid empty openings |

## 11. B2 Self-Check List

- [ ] Does my introduction end with a **clear thesis**?
- [ ] Does each body paragraph follow **PEEL**?
- [ ] Did I address the **other side** (and respond to it) where appropriate?
- [ ] Did I use **reference words** and **synonyms** to avoid repetition?
- [ ] Did I use at least **3 B2 structures** (passive, conditionals, participle clause, cleft)?
- [ ] Is my **register** consistent (no slang in formal text)?
- [ ] Did I **hedge** strong claims?
- [ ] Does my conclusion **restate** my position without adding new ideas?
