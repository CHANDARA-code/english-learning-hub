## Paragraph 1 — Working from Home (Nuanced Argument)

**Question:** *Remote working is often presented as an unqualified good for employees. To what extent do you agree?*

> The **widespread assumption** that remote working benefits all employees **equally** does not withstand close scrutiny. For experienced professionals with established networks and a dedicated workspace, **the elimination of** the commute undoubtedly **translates into** greater autonomy and, in many cases, higher output. **The picture is far less favourable, however, for** junior staff, **whose** development depends heavily on informal learning — the overheard conversation, the quick question at a colleague's desk, the chance to observe how senior colleagues handle difficult clients. **Deprived of** these opportunities, **new employees risk** stagnating professionally **even as** they appear productive on paper. **The benefits of remote work, in other words, are real but unevenly distributed**, and any policy that treats the workforce as homogeneous **is likely to** widen existing inequalities.

### 🔎 Analysis
| Technique | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Challenging an assumption** | *The widespread assumption that… does not withstand close scrutiny.* |
| **Nominalisation** | *the elimination of the commute* |
| **Contrast placed for emphasis** | *The picture is far less favourable, however, for…* |
| **Tricolon of concrete examples** | *the overheard conversation, the quick question…, the chance to observe…* |
| **Participle opening** | *Deprived of these opportunities,…* |
| **Reformulation signal** | *in other words* |
| **Implication** | *…is likely to widen existing inequalities.* |

Compare with the [B1](b1_examples.md#paragraph-1--working-from-home-opinion) and [B2](b2_examples.md#paragraph-1--working-from-home-peel) versions: the C1 paragraph doesn't just list pros and cons. It **qualifies** the claim (*for whom?*) and states an **implication**.

## Paragraph 2 — Urbanisation (Given → New Flow)

> Across Southeast Asia, the pace of urbanisation has **outstripped** the capacity of city governments to plan for it. **This mismatch** is most visible in housing: as rural migrants arrive in search of work, demand for affordable accommodation **far exceeds** supply. **The resulting shortfall** has pushed many newcomers into informal settlements on the urban periphery. **These settlements**, in turn, are often located on land prone to flooding, **precisely because** such land is the only space left undeveloped. **What begins as** an economic opportunity for migrants **can thus become** a source of acute vulnerability.

### 🔎 Analysis
Notice how **each sentence begins with information from the previous sentence** (given) and ends with something new:

| Sentence starts with (given) | …ends with (new) |
| :--- | :--- |
| *the pace of urbanisation* | *the capacity… to plan for it* |
| ***This mismatch*** | *housing / demand exceeds supply* |
| ***The resulting shortfall*** | *informal settlements on the periphery* |
| ***These settlements*** | *land prone to flooding* |
| ***What begins as*** *an economic opportunity* | *acute vulnerability* |

Very few "mechanical" connectors (*furthermore, moreover*) are needed — cohesion comes from **structure**.

## Paragraph 3 — Counter-argument + Rebuttal

> **Critics of** carbon taxation **contend that** it places a disproportionate burden on low-income households, **who** spend a larger share of their income on fuel and heating. **This objection carries considerable weight**; a poorly designed tax **could indeed** prove regressive. **Yet it is an argument against bad design rather than against the policy itself.** **Were** the revenue returned to citizens as an equal per-capita dividend — as **Canada's federal carbon pricing system did through its rebate scheme** — most low-income families would receive more than they pay, **since** their overall consumption, and hence their carbon footprint, is lower than that of wealthier households. **Far from** deepening inequality, **then**, a carefully structured carbon tax could **modestly reduce** it.

### 🔎 Analysis
- **Fair presentation of opposition:** *Critics of… contend that…* (reporting verb shows distance)
- **Concession with evaluation:** *This objection carries considerable weight*
- **Reframing rebuttal:** *an argument against bad design rather than against the policy itself*
- **Conditional inversion:** *Were the revenue returned…*
- **Real-world evidence** (Canada's rebate scheme)
- **Reversal:** *Far from deepening inequality,… could modestly reduce it.*

## Essay — Online Learning vs Classroom Learning (Argumentative)

**Question:** *"The rise of online education makes the physical classroom increasingly irrelevant." Critically evaluate this statement. (300–350 words)*

> **¶1 Introduction**
> The rapid expansion of online education, **accelerated** by pandemic-era school closures, has prompted **bold predictions** about the decline of the physical classroom. **Such claims, however, rest on a narrow conception of** what education is for. **While** digital platforms have **undeniably** transformed the **delivery** of content, they have proved far less capable of replicating the social and developmental functions of schooling. **The classroom is therefore not becoming irrelevant; rather, its role is being redefined.**
>
> **¶2 Concession**
> **It would be disingenuous to deny** the genuine advantages of online learning. For adult learners **balancing** work and family commitments, asynchronous courses offer a flexibility that fixed timetables cannot match. **Similarly,** students in remote areas can now access specialist instruction **that would previously have been** geographically out of reach. **In these contexts,** online provision **is not merely** a substitute **but** an improvement.
>
> **¶3 Main argument**
> **Where the argument falters is** in its assumption that education consists primarily in the **transmission** of information. **For children and adolescents in particular**, school is also where they learn to negotiate disagreement, collaborate with peers they did not choose, and sustain attention without constant stimulation. **The evidence from recent school closures is instructive**: studies in several countries documented not only measurable learning losses but also **a marked deterioration** in pupils' wellbeing, with disadvantaged students **disproportionately affected**. **Rarely has** the social dimension of schooling been **so clearly exposed by its absence.**
>
> **¶4 Conclusion**
> **The more productive question, then, is not whether** online learning will replace classrooms, **but how** the two can be combined. **If** schools were to delegate routine content delivery to digital tools, teachers **could devote** more classroom time to discussion, mentoring and collaborative work — precisely the activities that screens do least well. **Far from rendering the classroom obsolete, online education may ultimately clarify what it is uniquely able to offer.**

*(≈ 340 words)*

### 🔎 Analysis
| Paragraph | Move | C1 features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ¶1 | Reframes the issue; **qualified thesis** | *rest on a narrow conception of…; not…, rather…* |
| ¶2 | **Genuine concession** (strengthens credibility) | *It would be disingenuous to deny…; not merely… but…* |
| ¶3 | Core argument + evidence | *Where the argument falters is…* (cleft); *Rarely has…* (inversion); *disproportionately affected* |
| ¶4 | Reframes question + implication | *The more productive question is not whether… but how…* |

## Proposal — Improving Staff Wellbeing

**Task:** *Your manager has asked for a proposal on how to improve staff wellbeing in your company. (280–320 words)*

> ### Proposal: Improving Staff Wellbeing at Horizon Logistics
>
> **Aim**
> **The purpose of this proposal is to** outline practical measures to improve staff wellbeing, **in light of** the recent rise in sick leave and staff turnover.
>
> **Current situation**
> **Feedback gathered** through the annual staff survey **indicates that** employees' main concerns are excessive workload, a lack of flexibility, and limited opportunities for career development. **Of particular concern is** the finding that over a third of respondents reported feeling "frequently exhausted" at the end of the working week.
>
> **Proposals**
> *Flexible working:* **It is proposed that** staff be permitted to work from home up to two days per week, **subject to** team requirements. **This would** reduce commuting stress **without compromising** collaboration.
>
> *Workload review:* Department heads **should conduct** a quarterly review of workload distribution, **with a view to** identifying bottlenecks before they lead to burnout.
>
> *Professional development:* **A modest annual budget could be allocated** to each employee for training courses of their choice. **Not only would this** improve skills, **but it would also** demonstrate that the company values long-term growth.
>
> **Expected benefits**
> **Taken together,** these measures **are likely to** result in lower absenteeism, improved retention, and higher morale. **Although** there would be initial costs, **these should be weighed against** the considerable expense of recruiting and training replacement staff.
>
> **Conclusion**
> **It is strongly recommended that** these proposals be implemented on a six-month trial basis, **after which** their effectiveness could be evaluated through a follow-up survey.

### 🔎 Analysis
- **Genre conventions:** headings, future/hypothetical focus (*would, could*), impersonal tone
- **Subjunctive:** *It is proposed that staff **be** permitted… / It is strongly recommended that… **be** implemented*
- **Fronting:** *Of particular concern is…*
- **Inversion:** *Not only would this…*
- **Formal linking phrases:** *in light of, subject to, with a view to, taken together*

## Before & After: B2 → C1

### 🔴 B2 version
> Social media has many advantages, such as helping people stay in touch. However, it also has disadvantages. For example, many young people spend too much time on it, which can affect their mental health. Therefore, parents should control how much their children use it.

### 🟣 C1 version
> **The debate over** social media **tends to** focus on screen time, **yet** the **quantity** of use **may matter less than** its **nature**. Passive scrolling — **the endless consumption of** curated images of other people's lives — **has been associated with** heightened anxiety and low self-esteem among adolescents, **whereas** active use, such as messaging close friends, **appears to have** a neutral or even positive effect. **What this suggests is that** strict time limits imposed by parents **may prove less effective than** teaching young people to **recognise** which kinds of online activity leave them feeling worse.

**What changed:** reframing (*quantity vs nature*) · nominalisation · careful hedging (*has been associated with, appears to have*) · wh-cleft · precise contrast (*passive vs active use*) · more sophisticated recommendation.
