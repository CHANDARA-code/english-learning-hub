## Paragraph 1 — Working from Home (PEEL)

**Question:** *To what extent is working from home beneficial for employees?*

> **(P)** One of the most significant advantages of working from home is that it gives employees greater control over their time. **(E)** **Without** a daily commute, workers can reclaim hours that would otherwise be lost in traffic, **and** they can organise their day around their most productive periods. **(E)** A colleague of mine, **for instance**, now starts work at 7 a.m., finishes her most demanding tasks before lunch, and is able to collect her children from school — **something that would have been impossible** when she worked in the office. **(L)** **Therefore,** remote work **can** lead **not only** to higher productivity **but also** to a healthier balance between professional and family life.

### 🔎 Analysis
| PEEL step | Language |
| :--- | :--- |
| Point | *One of the most significant advantages of… is that…* |
| Explain | *Without a daily commute… that would otherwise be lost…* |
| Evidence | *A colleague of mine, for instance,…* + **third-conditional-style** *would have been impossible* |
| Link | *Therefore,… not only… but also…* |

**B2 features:** hedging (*can lead*), *not only… but also*, reference (*something that…*), collocations (*reclaim hours, demanding tasks*).

## Paragraph 2 — Public Transport (Cause & Effect)

> Rapid urbanisation in Southeast Asia **has led to** a dramatic increase in the number of private vehicles on the road. **As a consequence,** many capital cities now suffer from severe traffic congestion, **which** wastes time and damages the economy. **Furthermore,** the exhaust fumes produced by millions of motorbikes and cars **have been linked to** respiratory illnesses such as asthma. **If** governments **invested** more heavily in reliable bus and rail networks, **fewer** commuters **would** depend on private transport. **This**, in turn, **would** reduce **both** congestion **and** air pollution, **making** cities more pleasant places to live.

### 🔎 Analysis
- **Cause→effect chain:** *has led to → as a consequence → in turn*
- **Passive:** *have been linked to*
- **Second conditional:** *If governments invested…, fewer commuters would…*
- **Participle clause:** *…making cities more pleasant…*
- **Reference:** *This, in turn,…*

## Paragraph 3 — Counter-argument + Rebuttal

> **It is often argued that** banning mobile phones in schools would improve students' concentration. **Admittedly,** phones can be a serious distraction, **particularly** when students use social media during lessons. **However,** a complete ban **overlooks** the fact that smartphones are also powerful learning tools. Students can use them to look up information, record lectures, or practise languages with apps. **Rather than** banning phones entirely, **it would be more effective to** teach students how to use them responsibly, **since** this is a skill they will need throughout their lives.

### 🔎 Analysis
- **Present the other view:** *It is often argued that…*
- **Concede:** *Admittedly,…*
- **Rebut:** *However, a complete ban overlooks…*
- **Alternative solution:** *Rather than…, it would be more effective to…*

## Essay — Online Learning vs Classroom Learning (Discussion)

**Question:** *Some people believe that online learning will eventually replace traditional classrooms. Others think classrooms will always be necessary. Discuss both views and give your own opinion. (250 words)*

> **¶1 Introduction**
> Over the past decade, and especially since the COVID-19 pandemic, online education has grown **at an extraordinary rate**. **This has led some people to predict** that traditional classrooms will soon become obsolete. **While** I accept that online learning offers considerable flexibility, **I believe** that physical classrooms will remain essential, **particularly** for younger learners.
>
> **¶2 View A — online learning will replace classrooms**
> **Supporters of** online education **point out that** it is more accessible and affordable. Students in rural areas, **for example,** can attend courses at top universities **without having to** relocate, **and** learners can study at their own pace. **Moreover,** digital platforms allow teachers to reach thousands of students at once, **which** significantly reduces costs.
>
> **¶3 View B — classrooms will always be necessary**
> **On the other hand,** many educators argue that learning is not simply the transfer of information. In a classroom, students develop social skills, receive immediate feedback, and benefit from the discipline of a fixed routine. **Research** conducted during school closures **suggested that** many children fell behind **when** they studied only online, **largely because** they lacked motivation and direct support.
>
> **¶4 Conclusion**
> **In conclusion,** **although** online learning is a valuable addition to education, **it is unlikely to** replace classrooms completely. **In my view,** the most effective approach is a **blended model** that combines the convenience of online tools with the human interaction that only a classroom can provide.

*(≈ 255 words)*

### 🔎 Analysis
| Paragraph | Function | Key B2 techniques |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ¶1 | Background + paraphrase + **thesis** | *While I accept that…, I believe…* |
| ¶2 | View A with reasons & examples | *Supporters of… point out that… / without having to… / which…* |
| ¶3 | View B with evidence | *On the other hand… / Research… suggested that… / largely because…* |
| ¶4 | Balanced conclusion + solution | *it is unlikely to* (hedging) / *blended model* |

## Essay — Plastic Pollution (Problem & Solution)

**Question:** *Plastic waste is a growing problem in many countries. What are the causes, and what solutions can you suggest? (250 words)*

> **¶1** Plastic has become one of the most widely used materials in the modern world, **yet** its disposal **poses a serious threat to** the environment. This essay will examine the main causes of plastic pollution **before** suggesting some practical solutions.
>
> **¶2 Causes** The **primary** cause of the problem is our reliance on single-use plastics. Items such as bags, straws and food containers **are used** for only a few minutes **but** take hundreds of years to break down. **In addition,** many developing countries lack effective waste-management systems, **so** a large proportion of rubbish ends up in rivers and, eventually, the ocean. **A further factor is** a lack of public awareness; many people simply do not realise how damaging their daily habits are.
>
> **¶3 Solutions** **To tackle** the first cause, governments **could** introduce taxes on single-use plastics or ban them altogether, as Rwanda and Kenya have done with plastic bags. **Secondly,** investment in recycling facilities and waste collection **would** prevent rubbish from reaching waterways. **Finally,** schools and the media **should** run campaigns to **raise awareness** of the issue, **encouraging** people to carry reusable bags and bottles.
>
> **¶4** **To sum up,** plastic pollution **results from** a combination of consumer habits, poor infrastructure and limited awareness. **Although** no single measure will solve the problem, a combination of legislation, investment and education **could** make a significant difference.

### 🔎 Analysis
- **Signposting introduction:** *This essay will examine… before suggesting…*
- **Each solution matches a cause** (clear organisation)
- **Real-world example:** Rwanda and Kenya have banned plastic bags
- **Collocations:** *pose a threat, raise awareness, waste-management systems, single-use plastics*
- **Modals for suggestions:** *could, would, should*

## Formal Email — Complaint

**Task:** *You bought a laptop online, but it arrived damaged. Write an email to the company. (180–200 words)*

> **Subject:** Damaged laptop — Order #58214
>
> Dear Sir or Madam,
>
> **I am writing to complain about** a laptop **which** I ordered from your website on 3 September (Order #58214).
>
> **Although** the laptop arrived on time, the screen **was cracked** in the bottom-right corner, and the box **appeared to have been** badly damaged during delivery. **Furthermore,** the charger that **should have been** included was missing. I **have attached** photographs of both the laptop and the packaging **as evidence**.
>
> **I was particularly disappointed** **because** your website promises "secure packaging and careful handling", and I had paid an additional fee for express delivery.
>
> **I would therefore be grateful if you could** arrange for the laptop to be collected and **either** replaced with a new one **or** fully refunded, including the delivery charge. **I would appreciate it if** this **could be** resolved within the next seven days, **as** I need the computer for my work.
>
> **I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience.**
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Chan Dara

### 🔎 Analysis
- **Formal opening/closing:** *Dear Sir or Madam… Yours faithfully*
- **Passive and perfect passive:** *was cracked, appeared to have been damaged, should have been included*
- **Polite indirect requests:** *I would be grateful if you could… / I would appreciate it if…*
- **Clear structure:** reason → problem → why it matters → action requested → deadline

## Before & After: B1 → B2

### 🟡 B1 version
> I think working from home is good. Workers save time because they don't travel. They can also work in a quiet place. But some people feel lonely. So companies should think about this.

### 🔴 B2 version
> Working from home **offers clear benefits**, but it also **presents challenges** that employers **cannot afford to ignore**. **On the one hand,** eliminating the daily commute saves employees both time and money, **while** a quieter environment **allows** many people to concentrate better. **On the other hand,** **those who** live alone **may** experience isolation, **which** can gradually **affect** their motivation and mental health. **For this reason,** companies **should** consider hybrid arrangements **that** combine flexibility with regular face-to-face contact.

**What changed:** stronger topic sentence · *on the one hand / on the other hand* · relative clauses · hedging (*may*) · collocations · a specific recommendation.
