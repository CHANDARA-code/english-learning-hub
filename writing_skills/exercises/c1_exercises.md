## Part A — Sentence Skills

### Exercise 1: Nominalisation

Rewrite each sentence using the **noun form** of the underlined word(s). Start with the words given.

1. The government **decided** to raise taxes, and this **affected** small businesses badly.
   → *The government's ________*
2. Because unemployment **rose** sharply, crime **increased**.
   → *The sharp ________*
3. If we **analyse** the results carefully, we can see a clear pattern.
   → *Careful ________*
4. Cities are **expanding** rapidly, which **threatens** farmland.
   → *The rapid ________*
5. People **failed** to **recognise** the problem early, so it became worse.
   → *The ________*

---

### Exercise 2: Inversion and Emphasis

Rewrite using the words given.

1. The policy reduces costs and improves quality. *(Not only)*
2. If the government had acted sooner, the crisis could have been avoided. *(Had)*
3. We will see real change only when attitudes shift. *(Only when)*
4. A policy has rarely been so controversial. *(Rarely)*
5. If the situation worsens, emergency measures will be introduced. *(Should)*
6. People often overlook the cost to local communities. *(What)*
7. The rise in youth unemployment is particularly worrying. *(Particularly)*
8. As soon as the law was passed, protests began. *(No sooner)*

### Exercise 3: Precise Vocabulary

Replace the words in **bold** with a more precise C1 word or phrase.

1. Air pollution is a **big** problem in many cities.
2. The report **shows** that inequality is increasing.
3. The new law had a **bad** effect on small farmers.
4. Technology has **changed** the way we work.
5. Education is **very important** for development.
6. The new scheme aims to **help** people who have lost their jobs.
7. There are many **things** that affect student performance.
8. The number of tourists **went up a lot** last year.

### Exercise 4: Reporting Verbs and Stance

Choose the reporting verb that best fits the writer's attitude.

1. The study **(demonstrates / claims)** that exercise improves memory — its findings have been replicated many times.
2. The company **(asserts / confirms)** that its products are safe, but it has provided no independent evidence.
3. The author **(notes / alleges)** that the population has doubled since 1990. *(neutral)*
4. Critics **(contend / establish)** that the policy benefits only the wealthy. *(writer is not sure)*

Then rewrite sentence 2 to make the writer's doubt even clearer.

### Exercise 8: Upgrade a B2 Paragraph to C1

Rewrite this B2 paragraph at C1 level. Use at least: **1 nominalisation, 1 inversion or cleft, 2 precise vocabulary items, 1 hedged claim, and 1 implication**.

### Exercise 10: Write an Essay

Choose **one** question. Write **300–350 words**.

1. *"Artificial intelligence will create more jobs than it destroys." Critically evaluate this claim.*
2. *To what extent should governments restrict individual freedom in order to protect public health?*
3. *"Wealthy nations bear the greatest responsibility for tackling climate change." Discuss.*
4. *Some argue that universities should focus on preparing students for employment. Others believe their purpose is broader. Discuss and give your view.*

✔️ **Checklist:** qualified thesis · genuine concession · claim → reasoning → evidence · counter-argument + rebuttal · given → new flow · nominalisation (in balance) · at least one inversion/cleft · precise vocabulary · clear stance · conclusion with implication

### Exercise 11: Proposal or Report

Choose **one**. Write **280–320 words** with **headings**.

**Task A — Proposal:** *Your local council wants to make the town centre more attractive to young people. Write a proposal suggesting improvements and explaining the expected benefits.*

**Task B — Report:** *You have been asked to write a report on how effectively your college/company supports new students/employees in their first month. Describe current practice, evaluate it, and make recommendations.*

## ✅ Answer Key

### Exercise 1
1. The government's **decision** to raise taxes had a **detrimental effect / adverse impact** on small businesses.
2. The sharp **rise** in unemployment led to an **increase** in crime.
3. Careful **analysis** of the results reveals a clear pattern.
4. The rapid **expansion** of cities **poses a threat** to farmland.
5. The **failure** to **recognise** the problem early led to its **deterioration / escalation**. *(or: The failure to recognise the problem early allowed it to worsen.)*

### Exercise 2
1. **Not only does** the policy reduce costs, **but** it also improves quality.
2. **Had** the government acted sooner, the crisis could have been avoided.
3. **Only when** attitudes shift **will we see** real change.
4. **Rarely has** a policy been so controversial.
5. **Should** the situation worsen, emergency measures will be introduced.
6. **What** people often overlook **is** the cost to local communities.
7. **Particularly worrying is** the rise in youth unemployment.
8. **No sooner had** the law **been passed than** protests began.

### Exercise 3 (suggested answers)
1. a **pressing / pervasive / acute** problem
2. **demonstrates / reveals / indicates**
3. an **adverse / detrimental** effect
4. **transformed / reshaped**
5. **essential / pivotal / indispensable**
6. **support / assist**
7. **factors**
8. **rose sharply / increased markedly**

### Exercise 4
1. **demonstrates** (strong, supported evidence)
2. **asserts** (writer is sceptical)
3. **notes** (neutral)
4. **contend** (writer is distancing themselves)

Stronger doubt: *The company **asserts** that its products are safe, **a claim that remains unsupported by** any independent evidence.*

### Exercise 5 (suggested answer)
> Many young people are moving to cities in search of work. **This migration** leaves rural areas with an ageing population. **An ageing workforce**, however, cannot sustain heavy agricultural labour, and farm productivity is consequently declining. **This decline** is, in turn, one factor behind rising food prices in the very cities to which the young have moved.

### Exercise 6 (suggested answers)
1. *Social media is harmful to young people **only insofar as** it replaces, rather than supplements, face-to-face relationships; **the real issue is therefore not** access **but** the design of platforms that reward compulsive use.*
2. *Banning cars from city centres is justified **provided that** reliable and affordable public transport is available first, **since** otherwise the policy would disproportionately penalise those with no alternative.*
3. ***Although** tourism generates vital income for developing countries, its benefits are sustainable **only insofar as** revenue reaches local communities and environmental limits are respected.*

### Exercise 7 (suggested answers)
1. **Expose an assumption:** *This objection **assumes that** people work primarily for survival; yet evidence from basic income pilots suggests that most recipients continue working, often using the security to seek better jobs or retrain.*
2. **Outweigh it:** *Even granting higher initial costs, the long-term savings from reduced fuel imports and avoided climate damage **far exceed** the upfront investment — particularly as the price of solar and wind power has fallen dramatically over the past decade.*
3. **Limit it:** *Tests may be objective **in their marking**, but they are hardly objective **in what they measure**, since performance is heavily influenced by access to private tutoring.*
4. **Turn it:** *Paradoxically, the isolation of remote learning **has highlighted** the social value of schools, strengthening the case for designing classrooms around collaboration rather than lectures.*

### Exercise 8 — Model Answer
> **The appeal of** fast fashion **lies in** its affordability, **yet** this very affordability **drives a cycle of overconsumption** in which garments are worn only a handful of times before being discarded. **The environmental consequences** are considerable: **the accumulation of** textile waste in landfill, much of it made from non-biodegradable synthetic fibres, **represents** a problem that recycling schemes have so far **failed to address**. **Equally troubling are** the labour conditions in many garment-producing countries, where workers **are frequently paid** well below a living wage. **Defenders of** the industry **contend that** it provides employment in economies with few alternatives — a point **with some merit**. **What this argument overlooks, however, is that** the choice is not between exploitative jobs and no jobs, **but between** current practices and **fairer** ones. **The implication is that** responsibility **cannot rest with** consumers alone; **binding** standards for brands **are likely to prove** far more effective than voluntary pledges.

**Checklist:** nominalisation ✔️ (*the accumulation of, the environmental consequences*) · fronting/cleft ✔️ (*Equally troubling are… / What this argument overlooks… is*) · precise vocabulary ✔️ (*overconsumption, non-biodegradable, binding*) · hedging ✔️ (*are likely to prove*) · implication ✔️

### Exercise 9 — Sample Plan
| Section | Notes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Qualified thesis | *Growth is a necessary but insufficient priority; without investment in health, education and institutions, its gains are neither shared nor sustained.* |
| Concession | *Growth has historically been the most powerful driver of poverty reduction (e.g., East Asia).* |
| Argument 1 | *Growth without distribution → inequality → social instability; evidence: rising inequality in some fast-growing economies.* |
| Argument 2 | *Human capital (education, health) is itself a precondition for long-term growth.* |
| Counter + rebuttal | *"Poor countries can't afford social spending." → **Expose assumption**: treats it as a cost rather than an investment.* |
| Conclusion | *The real question is not growth vs welfare but the kind of growth pursued; implication for aid and policy.* |

### Exercise 10 — Model Answer (Question 1)
> **¶1** Every major technological shift has been accompanied by fears of mass unemployment, and every time, the labour market has eventually adapted. **It is tempting, therefore, to assume** that artificial intelligence will follow the same pattern. **Such optimism, however, rests on a historical analogy that may not hold.** While AI **is likely to** generate new forms of work, the claim that it will create **more** jobs than it destroys **overlooks** both the speed of the transition and the question of **who** will benefit.
>
> **¶2** **It would be unwise to dismiss** the optimistic case entirely. Past innovations, from mechanised agriculture to the personal computer, eliminated whole categories of employment while creating industries that no one could have foreseen. AI **is already producing** demand for data specialists, system trainers and ethicists, and **its capacity to** raise productivity **could**, in principle, lower costs and stimulate demand across the economy.
>
> **¶3** **Where the analogy breaks down is** in the **scope** of the technology. Earlier machines replaced physical or routine tasks; AI **increasingly encroaches upon** cognitive work once considered secure, from translation to legal research. **Moreover,** the new roles it creates **tend to require** advanced skills, **leaving** displaced workers — **particularly** older and less-educated employees — poorly placed to take them up. **The net figure, in other words, may conceal a profound redistribution of opportunity.**
>
> **¶4** **Ultimately,** whether AI creates or destroys more jobs **is less a technological question than a political one.** **Only if** governments invest seriously in retraining, and **only if** the productivity gains are shared through wages and taxation, **will** the optimistic prediction **stand a realistic chance of** being fulfilled. **Left to market forces alone,** the transition **may well** produce more jobs overall **while** leaving a great many people worse off.
>
> *(≈ 335 words)*

### Exercise 11 — Model Answer (Task A)
> ### Proposal: Revitalising the Town Centre for Young People
>
> **Aim**
> The purpose of this proposal is to recommend measures that would make the town centre more appealing to residents aged 15–25, who, according to a recent council survey, visit it far less often than other age groups.
>
> **Current situation**
> At present, the town centre offers little beyond retail outlets, most of which close at 6 p.m. **Of particular concern is** the absence of affordable spaces where young people can meet without being expected to spend money. **As a result,** many choose to travel to neighbouring cities or remain at home.
>
> **Proposals**
> *Community hub:* **It is proposed that** the vacant library building **be converted** into a multi-purpose youth hub offering free Wi-Fi, study areas and a small performance space. **This would provide** a safe, non-commercial meeting place.
>
> *Evening events:* The council **could organise** a monthly evening market featuring local food vendors, live music and stalls run by young entrepreneurs. **Not only would this** attract visitors, **but it would also** support small local businesses.
>
> *Improved transport:* Extending bus services until 11 p.m. on Fridays and Saturdays **would enable** young people from outlying areas to attend events safely.
>
> **Expected benefits**
> **Taken together,** these measures **are likely to** increase footfall in the evenings, reduce antisocial behaviour **by providing** constructive alternatives, and strengthen young people's sense of belonging. **While** the hub would require initial investment, **these costs should be weighed against** the long-term economic benefits of a livelier town centre.
>
> **Conclusion**
> **It is strongly recommended that** the council pilot these proposals for one year, **with** youth representatives **involved in** both planning and evaluation.
>
> *(≈ 300 words)*
