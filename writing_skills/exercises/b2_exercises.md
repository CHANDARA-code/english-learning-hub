## Part A — Sentence Skills

### Exercise 1: Advanced Connectors

Choose the correct option.

1. ________ the high price, the product sold very well. *(Although / Despite)*
2. The city has grown rapidly. ________, housing has become very expensive. *(Consequently / Whereas)*
3. Some people prefer cities, ________ others prefer the countryside. *(whereas / moreover)*
4. ________ being tired, she continued working. *(In spite of / Even though)*
5. The flight was cancelled ________ bad weather. *(because / due to)*
6. It is cheap. ________, it is environmentally friendly. *(Furthermore / Nevertheless)*
7. The plan is risky. ________, it is worth trying. *(Therefore / Nevertheless)*
8. ________ the government acts quickly, the problem will get worse. *(Unless / Provided)*

---

### Exercise 2: Sentence Transformation

Rewrite each sentence using the word in brackets. Keep the same meaning.

1. People throw away millions of tonnes of food every year. *(are)*
2. I didn't know about the meeting, so I didn't attend. *(If)*
3. Governments don't invest enough in schools, so many students drop out. *(would)*
4. The cost worries me most. *(What)*
5. After she had finished the report, she sent it to her manager. *(Having)*
6. The bus is cheaper than the taxi, and it is also more environmentally friendly. *(not only)*
7. Although it was raining heavily, the match continued. *(despite)*
8. The museum is in the old town. It attracts thousands of visitors. *(which)*

---

### Exercise 3: Informal → Formal

Rewrite each sentence in a formal style.

1. Loads of kids these days are really into video games.
2. The government needs to sort out the traffic problem ASAP.
3. Prices have gone up a lot, so people can't buy as much stuff.
4. I think we should find out why so many students are dropping out.
5. You can't just ignore climate change — it's a massive deal!

### Exercise 4: Hedge the Claim

Make each statement less absolute (more academic).

1. Video games make children violent.
2. Everyone prefers online shopping now.
3. This policy will end unemployment.
4. Fast food causes obesity.
5. All teenagers are addicted to their phones.

## Part B — Paragraph Skills

### Exercise 5: Label the PEEL Paragraph

Label each sentence **P** (Point), **E1** (Explain), **E2** (Evidence/Example), or **L** (Link).

> **(a)** For instance, a study by the World Health Organization estimated that air pollution causes millions of premature deaths every year. **(b)** Reducing car use in cities would significantly improve public health. **(c)** Consequently, limiting private vehicles should be a priority for city governments. **(d)** This is because vehicle emissions are one of the main sources of harmful air pollutants in urban areas.

Correct order: ___ → ___ → ___ → ___

## Part C — Essay & Genre Skills

### Exercise 10: Identify the Essay Type

Match each question to the essay type: **Opinion · Discussion · Advantages/Disadvantages · Problem/Solution**

1. *Many young people are unemployed. Why is this, and what can be done?*
2. *Some say that zoos protect animals; others say they are cruel. Discuss both views and give your opinion.*
3. *Online shopping is replacing traditional shops. What are the advantages and disadvantages?*
4. *Children should start school at age seven rather than five. To what extent do you agree?*

---

### Exercise 11: Write an Essay

Choose **one** question. Write **250 words** (4–5 paragraphs).

1. *Some people think that tourists should pay more than local people to visit museums and historic sites. To what extent do you agree?* (Opinion)
2. *Some believe that governments should spend money on public transport, while others think they should build more roads. Discuss both views and give your opinion.* (Discussion)
3. *More and more people are working from home. What are the advantages and disadvantages?* (Adv/Disadv)
4. *Many cities have serious air pollution. What are the causes, and what solutions can you suggest?* (Problem/Solution)

✔️ **Checklist:** thesis at end of introduction · PEEL body paragraphs · both sides addressed · 3+ B2 structures (passive, conditional, participle, cleft) · formal register · hedging · conclusion without new ideas

## ✅ Answer Key

### Exercise 1
1. Despite · 2. Consequently · 3. whereas · 4. In spite of · 5. due to · 6. Furthermore · 7. Nevertheless · 8. Unless

### Exercise 2
1. Millions of tonnes of food **are thrown away** every year.
2. **If** I **had known** about the meeting, I **would have attended**.
3. **If** governments **invested** more in schools, fewer students **would** drop out.
4. **What** worries me most is the cost.
5. **Having** finished the report, she sent it to her manager.
6. The bus is **not only** cheaper than the taxi **but also** more environmentally friendly.
7. The match continued **despite** the heavy rain.
8. The museum, **which** is in the old town, attracts thousands of visitors.

### Exercise 3 (suggested answers)
1. A large number of children today are very interested in video games.
2. The government needs to address the traffic problem as soon as possible.
3. Prices have risen significantly, so consumers are able to purchase fewer goods.
4. It is important to investigate why so many students are leaving education early.
5. Climate change is a serious issue that cannot be ignored.

### Exercise 4 (suggested answers)
1. Some violent video games **may contribute to** aggressive behaviour in **certain** children.
2. **An increasing number of** people **appear to** prefer online shopping.
3. This policy **is likely to reduce** unemployment.
4. **Regular consumption of** fast food **can contribute to** obesity.
5. **Many** teenagers **tend to** spend excessive time on their phones.

### Exercise 5
**b (P) → d (E1) → a (E2) → c (L)**

### Exercise 6 (suggested answers)
1. *Although tourism brings economic benefits to local communities, I believe that uncontrolled tourism damages the environment and local culture, so it should be carefully regulated.*
2. *While studying abroad can be expensive and emotionally challenging, I would argue that the academic and personal benefits it offers far outweigh these difficulties.*
3. *Although fast food is convenient and affordable, regular consumption can lead to serious health problems, so governments should do more to promote healthier alternatives.*

### Exercise 7 (suggested answer)
> Plastic bags are a **serious environmental problem**. **They** pollute the ocean and harm marine life. **Sea creatures** such as turtles often swallow **them** because **they** mistake **the bags** for food. **For this reason,** governments should ban **single-use plastic bags**, as **such a measure** would significantly reduce **this form of** pollution.

### Exercise 8 — Model Answer (Topic 1)
> **(P)** One of the greatest advantages of learning a second language is that it significantly improves career prospects. **(E)** In an increasingly globalised economy, employers value staff who can communicate with international clients and partners, which makes bilingual candidates more competitive. **(E)** In Cambodia, for example, graduates who speak fluent English or Chinese are often offered higher salaries in sectors such as tourism, banking and international trade. **(L)** Therefore, investing time in learning another language can bring long-term professional and financial rewards. *(≈ 90 words)*

### Exercise 9 (suggested answers)
1. (a) *Critics argue that free university education is too expensive for governments.* (b) *However, a more educated workforce generates higher tax revenue in the long term, which can offset the initial cost.*
2. (a) *It is often claimed that tourism damages local cultures and the environment.* (b) *Nevertheless, with proper regulation, tourism can fund conservation projects and help preserve traditional crafts.*
3. (a) *Some people believe a four-day week would reduce productivity.* (b) *However, trials in several countries have suggested that rested employees are often more productive per hour.*

### Exercise 11 — Model Answer (Question 1)
> Many countries charge international tourists higher entrance fees than local residents at museums and historic sites. **Although** this policy might seem unfair to visitors, **I believe** it is largely justified, **as** it helps to fund conservation and ensures that heritage remains accessible to local people.
>
> **The main argument in favour of** dual pricing is financial. Maintaining ancient monuments **is extremely costly**, and in developing countries public funding is often limited. **By charging** foreign visitors more, authorities can raise the revenue needed for restoration and protection **without placing** a heavy burden on local citizens. **At Angkor Wat, for instance,** foreign visitors pay an entrance fee while Cambodian citizens enter free of charge, and ticket revenue helps to support the site's upkeep.
>
> **Furthermore,** local residents **have usually already contributed** to the upkeep of these sites through taxes. It **therefore** seems reasonable that **those who** have not made such a contribution should pay a higher price. **Lower prices for locals** also encourage citizens, **particularly** young people, to learn about their own history and culture.
>
> **Admittedly,** some tourists **may feel** they are being treated unfairly, **and** very high prices **could discourage** visitors. **However,** **provided that** the difference in price is reasonable, most travellers **appear willing** to pay a little extra to support the places they visit.
>
> **In conclusion,** **while** dual pricing should not be excessive, it is a fair way of funding heritage protection and ensuring that local people can enjoy their own cultural treasures.
>
> *(≈ 260 words)*

### Exercise 12 — Model Answer (Task A)
> Dear Sir or Madam,
>
> I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with my recent stay at your hotel from 14 to 17 August (booking reference HB-3391).
>
> When I booked the room, your website advertised a fully functioning swimming pool, spotless rooms and "attentive, friendly service". Unfortunately, my experience did not match this description. On arrival, I was informed that the pool had been closed for repairs, although no warning had been given at the time of booking. Furthermore, the room had clearly not been cleaned properly: there was dust on the furniture and the bathroom floor was stained.
>
> When I reported these problems to reception, the staff were unhelpful and simply told me that nothing could be done. I was particularly disappointed by this, as I had chosen your hotel specifically because of its facilities and excellent reviews.
>
> In view of these problems, I would be grateful if you could offer me a partial refund of at least 30% of the total cost. I would also appreciate an explanation of why guests were not informed that the pool was closed.
>
> I look forward to your prompt reply.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Rotha Keo
>
> *(≈ 205 words)*
