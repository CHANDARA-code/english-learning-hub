2. If Keynesian principles are applied correctly, fiscal stimulus can break the cycle of declining demand and rising unemployment.
3. Critics of Keynesian economics argue that government intervention crowds out private investment and creates dependency on public spending.

### 3967. supply-side economics /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** សេដ្ឋកិច្ចផ្នែកការផ្គត់ផ្គង់
1. Supply-side economics focuses on increasing productive capacity through tax cuts, deregulation, and investment in human and physical capital.
2. If supply-side policies succeed in boosting productivity, the economy can grow faster without triggering inflationary pressures.
3. The effectiveness of supply-side economics remains debated, with critics arguing that its benefits are often concentrated among higher earners.

---

### 3968. aggregate demand /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** តម្រូវការសរុប
1. Aggregate demand represents the total demand for goods and services in an economy at a given overall price level.
2. If aggregate demand falls sharply, businesses will reduce output and employment, potentially triggering a broader economic downturn.
3. Fiscal and monetary stimulus is typically used to boost aggregate demand during recessions and prevent a deflationary spiral.

---

### 3969. aggregate supply /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ការផ្គត់ផ្គង់សរុប
1. Aggregate supply represents the total amount of goods and services that all producers in an economy are willing to supply at different price levels.
2. If a supply shock reduces aggregate supply, it can lead to both rising prices and falling output, a combination known as stagflation.
3. Long-run aggregate supply is determined by factors such as the quantity of labor, capital, and the state of technology in an economy.

---

### 3970. multiplier effect /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ផលប៉ះពាល់ចម្រុះ
1. The multiplier effect means that an initial increase in government spending can lead to a larger overall increase in national income.
2. If the marginal propensity to consume is high, the multiplier effect of a fiscal stimulus will be correspondingly larger.
3. Understanding the multiplier effect is important for policymakers seeking to design fiscal packages that provide maximum economic stimulus.

---

### 3971. crowding out /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ការដេញចេញ
1. Crowding out occurs when government borrowing raises interest rates and reduces private investment by making credit more expensive.
2. If crowding out is significant, the net economic impact of fiscal stimulus may be much smaller than the initial government expenditure.
3. Critics of expansionary fiscal policy often cite the crowding-out effect as a reason to prefer monetary policy as a stimulus tool.

---

### 3972. velocity of money /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:**ល្បឿនចរចាររបស់ប្រាក់
1. The velocity of money measures how frequently a unit of currency is used in transactions within a given period of time.
2. If the velocity of money falls, it may indicate that people are hoarding cash rather than spending, which can slow economic activity.
3. The quantity theory of money links the money supply, velocity, price level, and output to explain inflationary pressures.

---

### 3973. money supply /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** បរិមាណប្រាក់
1. Central banks control the money supply through open market operations, reserve requirements, and the setting of policy interest rates.
2. If the money supply grows faster than economic output, it tends to create inflationary pressures that erode purchasing power.
3. The relationship between money supply and inflation is central to the monetarist school of economic thought pioneered by Milton Friedman.

### 3974. liquidity /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ភាពងាយស្រួលក្នុងការប្ដូរជាសាច់ប្រាក់
1. Liquidity refers to the ease with which an asset can be converted into cash without significantly affecting its market price.
2. If a bank faces a sudden liquidity crisis, it may be forced to sell assets at distressed prices, amplifying its financial difficulties.
3. Central banks act as lenders of last resort, providing liquidity to the financial system when markets seize up in times of stress.

### 3975. solvency /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ភាពមានលទ្ធភាពបង់បំណុល
1. A company is considered solvent if the total value of its assets exceeds its total liabilities, meaning it can meet its long-term obligations.
2. If the solvency of a major bank is called into question, it can trigger a run on deposits that quickly becomes a self-fulfilling crisis.
3. Regulators require banks to maintain minimum capital ratios to ensure their solvency under adverse economic scenarios.

---

### 3976. bank run /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ការរត់ប្រញាប់ដកប្រាក់ពីធនាគារ
1. A bank run occurs when large numbers of depositors simultaneously withdraw their money out of fear that the bank may become insolvent.
2. If a bank run is not stopped quickly, it can become self-fulfilling as withdrawals drain the bank's liquidity and force its closure.
3. Deposit insurance schemes are designed to prevent bank runs by assuring depositors that their savings are protected up to a certain limit.

### 3977. credit crunch /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ភាពត្រឹមតែនៃឥណទាន
1. A credit crunch occurs when banks become unwilling or unable to lend, starving businesses and households of the credit they need to operate.
2. If a credit crunch persists, it can cause a severe economic contraction as investment and consumption both fall simultaneously.
3. The credit crunch of 2008 led to the near-collapse of the global financial system and triggered the worst recession in decades.

### 3978. repo rate /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** អត្រាការប្រាក់ REPO
1. The central bank raised its repo rate to signal its intention to tighten monetary conditions and reduce inflationary pressures.
2. If the repo rate is cut, commercial banks can borrow more cheaply from the central bank, which typically leads to lower lending rates.
3. The repo rate is one of the most powerful tools available to central banks for influencing short-term market interest rates.

### 3979. base rate /noun/

1. The base rate set by the central bank influences the interest rates that commercial banks charge their customers for loans.
2. If the base rate is raised significantly, homeowners with variable-rate mortgages will see their monthly payments increase substantially.
3. A prolonged period of near-zero base rates encouraged excessive borrowing and risk-taking in many parts of the financial system.

### 3980. yield curve /noun/

1. The yield curve plots the interest rates on bonds of the same credit quality but different maturities, from short to long term.
2. If the yield curve inverts, meaning short-term rates exceed long-term rates, it is often seen as a reliable predictor of recession.
3. A steep yield curve generally reflects expectations of stronger economic growth and higher inflation in the future.

### 3981. quantitative tightening /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** ការដកទ្រព្យចេញពីប្រព័ន្ធ
1. Quantitative tightening involves the central bank reducing its balance sheet by selling bonds or allowing them to mature without reinvestment.
2. If quantitative tightening is implemented too aggressively, it may drain liquidity from financial markets and push up interest rates sharply.
3. Central banks must carefully manage the pace of quantitative tightening to avoid disrupting markets or triggering a financial market sell-off.

### 3982. open market operation /noun/

1. Open market operations involve the central bank buying or selling government securities to influence the supply of money in the economy.
2. If the central bank conducts open market operations by purchasing bonds, it injects liquidity into the banking system and lowers interest rates.
3. Open market operations are the primary tool through which central banks in most advanced economies implement monetary policy decisions.

### 3983. reserve requirement /noun/
**ន័យខ្មែរ:** តម្រូវការទុកប្រាក់បម្រុង
