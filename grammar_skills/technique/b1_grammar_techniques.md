## 2. Tense System: Past Simple vs Present Perfect

This is the most critical distinction tested at B1.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PAST SIMPLE: Finished time in the past (yesterday, in 2021)   │
│              Formula: Subject + Verb-2                       │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PRESENT PERFECT: Unfinished time / Life experience / Result  │
│                  Formula: Subject + have/has + Past Participle│
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Direct Comparison

| Feature | Past Simple (អតីតកាលធម្មតា) | Present Perfect (បច្ចុប្បន្នកាលបរិបូណ៌) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time Marker** | *yesterday, last week, in 2018, ago, when I was young* | *ever, never, already, yet, just, for, since, recently* |
| **Connection to Present**| Dead/finished past; no direct link to now | Action in past has a result or continuation in the present |
| **Example** | *I **lost** my keys yesterday (I might have found them now).* | *I **have lost** my keys! (I cannot enter my home right now).* |
| **Question** | *Did you visit Angkor Wat last year?* | *Have you ever visited Angkor Wat?* |

### Since vs For
- **Since + starting point in time:** *since 2015, since Monday, since 9:00 AM, since I was a child.*
- **For + duration of time:** *for five years, for two hours, for three days, for a long time.*

## 3. Past Continuous vs Past Simple (Narrative Actions)

Used together to tell stories when a long background action is interrupted by a short event.

$$\text{Long Action [Past Continuous]} + \mathbf{WHEN} + \text{Short Action [Past Simple]}$$
$$\mathbf{WHILE} + \text{Long Action [Past Continuous]}, + \text{Short Action [Past Simple]}$$

- *I **was studying** in my room when the electricity **went** off.*
- *While we **were walking** to the market, it **started** to rain heavily.*
- ⚠️ **Trap:** Do not use continuous form with state verbs (*know, believe, understand, want, like*):
  - ❌ *I was knowing him for years.* 
  - ✅ *I **knew** him for years.* / *I **have known** him for years.*

## 4. Future Forms: Will vs Be Going To vs Present Continuous

English does not have one single future tense; choose based on **how the decision was made**:

| Future Form | Core Function | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Be Going To + Verb** | Intentions & prior plans (សម្រេចចិត្តរួចមុនពេលនិយាយ) | *I **am going to buy** a new laptop next month.* |
| **Be Going To + Verb** | Prediction based on physical evidence now | *Look at those dark clouds! It **is going to rain**.* |
| **Will + Verb** | Instant spontaneous decision (សម្រេចចិត្តភ្លាមៗ) | *The phone is ringing. I **will answer** it!* |
| **Will + Verb** | General opinion/promise/belief | *I think you **will pass** the examination easily.* |
| **Present Continuous** | Fixed calendar arrangement with other people | *I **am meeting** the doctor tomorrow at 3:00 PM.* |

## 5. Modals of Obligation, Advice & Ability

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   DEGREES OF MODALITY                  │
├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┤
│ 100% Obligation          │ must / have to              │
│ 0% Obligation (No need)  │ don't have to / needn't     │
│ Prohibition (Forbidden!) │ mustn't / cannot            │
│ Recommendation / Advice  │ should / ought to           │
│ Ability (Present / Past) │ can / could / be able to    │
└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┘
```

### Critical Contrast: Mustn't vs Don't Have To
- **Mustn't (ហាមដាច់ខាត):** You are forbidden to do it.  
  *You **mustn't smoke** inside the hospital.*
- **Don't have to (មិនចាំបាច់ធ្វើទេ ប៉ុន្តែចង់ធ្វើក៏បាន):** Lack of obligation; optional.  
  *Tomorrow is Sunday, so I **don't have to wake up** early.*

## 6. Conditionals: Zero, First & Second Conditionals

| Conditional | Condition Clause (If-clause) | Result Clause | Function & Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zero** | *If + Present Simple* | *Present Simple* | Universal scientific fact, general truth  <br>*(If you heat ice, it **melts**.)* |
| **First** | *If + Present Simple* | *will / can + Infinitive* | Real, likely future possibility  <br>*(If you study hard, you **will pass**.)* |
| **Second**| *If + Past Simple* | *would / could + Infinitive*| Unreal, hypothetical, or imaginary situation  <br>*(If I won a million dollars, I **would travel** the world.)* |

> 💡 **B1 Form Rule:** In Second Conditional, use **were** for all persons in formal English:  
> *If I **were** you, I would consult a doctor immediately.* (ប្រសិនបើខ្ញុំជាអ្នក...)

## 7. The Passive Voice: Present & Past Simple

Use the passive when the **action/object** is more important than the doer, or when the doer is unknown.

$$\text{Active: } \text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object}$$
$$\text{Passive: } \text{Object} + \mathbf{BE} + \mathbf{Past\ Participle\ (V3)} + [\text{by Agent}]$$

| Tense | Active Form | Passive Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Simple** | *Farmers grow rice in Battambang.* | *Rice **is grown** in Battambang (by farmers).* |
| **Past Simple** | *King Suryavarman II built Angkor Wat.* | *Angkor Wat **was built** by King Suryavarman II.* |
| **Negative Present**| *They do not allow cameras here.* | *Cameras **are not allowed** here.* |
| **Passive Question**| *Did they deliver the parcel?* | ***Was** the parcel **delivered**?* |

## 8. Defining Relative Clauses (who, which, that, where, whose)

Connect two ideas into one clear sentence without repeating nouns.

| Relative Pronoun | Used For | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **who / that** | People | *The teacher **who** taught me English was very patient.* |
| **which / that** | Things & Animals | *This is the smartphone **which** I bought yesterday.* |
| **where** | Places | *Siem Reap is the city **where** Angkor Wat is located.* |
| **whose** | Possessions (របស់នរណា) | *I met a student **whose** brother is a famous singer.* |

> ⚠️ **Omission Rule:** You can omit *who/which/that* when it is the **object** of the relative clause:  
> *The book **(that)** I am reading is fascinating.* (Subject = *I*; Object = *The book* → *that* is optional).

## 9. Gerunds vs Infinitives (Verb Patterns)

Some verbs are followed by **-ing (Gerund)**, while others require **to + Infinitive**:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ VERB + GERUND (-ing)                 │ VERB + INFINITIVE (to + verb)       │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ enjoy, avoid, mind, practice,        │ decide, plan, hope, promise, agree, │
│ suggest, consider, finish, keep      │ refuse, manage, want, need, afford  │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ *She **enjoys reading** novels.*     │ *They **decided to travel** abroad.*│
│ *He **avoided answering** my email.* │ *I **hope to see** you soon.*       │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Prepositions ALWAYS take Gerunds
Any verb placed directly after a preposition (*in, on, at, about, without, before, after*) **must** take `-ing`:
- *Thank you **for helping** me.* (NOT: *for to help*)
- *She left **without saying** goodbye.*
- *He is interested **in learning** coding.*

## 10. Comparatives, Superlatives & Equality

| Structure | Pattern | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Short Adjectives** | Adjective + *-er than* | *Phnom Penh is **busier than** Kampot.* |
| **Long Adjectives** | *more + Adjective + than* | *This exam was **more difficult than** the last one.* |
| **Superlative** | *the + -est / the most + Adj* | *Mount Everest is **the highest** mountain in the world.* |
| **Equality (ដូចគ្នា)** | *as + Adjective + as* | *His English is **as fluent as** a native speaker's.* |
| **Negative Equality** | *not as / so + Adjective + as* | *Bus travel is **not as fast as** the bullet train.* |
| **Excess / Deficiency** | *too + Adj / Adj + enough* | *The tea is **too hot to drink**.* / *He isn't **old enough**.* |

## 11. Common B1 Grammar Mistakes & Fixes

| ❌ Common Error | ✅ Correct B1 Structure | Why It's Wrong & Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *I am agree with you.* | *I **agree** with you.* | *Agree* is a verb, not an adjective! |
| *She didn't went to school.* | *She didn't **go** to school.* | After auxiliary *did/didn't*, use base verb (V1). |
| *I have seen him yesterday.* | *I **saw** him yesterday.* | *Yesterday* specifies a finished past time; use Past Simple. |
| *Although it rained, but we went.* | *Although it rained, we went.* | Never use *Although* and *but* in the same sentence. |
| *Because I was tired, so I slept.* | *Because I was tired, I slept.* | Do not combine *because* and *so* across two clauses. |
| *He don't like coffee.* | *He **doesn't** like coffee.* | Third person singular (*he/she/it*) takes *doesn't*. |
| *Every students are here.* | *Every **student is** here.* | *Every* is followed by singular countable noun + singular verb. |

- [ ] Do I correctly use *-ing* after all prepositions without hesitation?

- [ ] Do I avoid double connectors (*Although... but*, *Because... so*)?
