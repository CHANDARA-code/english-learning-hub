## 1. From B1 to B2: What Changes

| Area | B1 Level | B2 Level | ន័យខ្មែរ (ការវិវឌ្ឍ) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deduction** | Simple facts (*I am sure he is at home*) | Modal deduction (*He **must have missed** the bus*) | ការទាញហេតុផល និងស្មានរឿងអតីត |
| **Conditionals** | 1st & 2nd (*If I have money, I will buy*) | 3rd & Mixed (*If I **had studied**, I **would be** a doctor now*) | លក្ខខណ្ឌកាត់កាល និងលទ្ធផលបច្ចុប្បន្ន |
| **Passives** | Basic action (*The car was repaired*) | Impersonal reporting (*It **is widely believed to have been** caused by...*) | ល្បះអកម្មរាយការណ៍ព័ត៌មានបែបផ្លូវការ |
| **Connectors** | *but, because, so* | *despite, in spite of, nevertheless, whereas, in order that* | ពាក្យភ្ជាប់ស្មុគស្មាញ និងទម្រង់កិរិយាសព្ទភ្ជាប់ |
| **Perspective** | Personal voice | Nuanced, objective, academic distance | ភាសាបែបអព្យាក្រឹត្យ និងសិក្សាស្រាវជ្រាវ |

## 2. Aspectual Contrast: Present Perfect Simple vs Continuous

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PRESENT PERFECT CONTINUOUS (have/has been + V-ing)                     │
│ Focus on ACTIVITY, DURATION, or TEMPORARY PROCESS (How long?)          │
│ *I have been painting the living room all morning.* (It is still wet)  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PRESENT PERFECT SIMPLE (have/has + V3)                                 │
│ Focus on COMPLETION, RESULT, or QUANTITY (How many / How much?)        │
│ *I have painted three walls already.* (Those three are finished)       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- ⚠️ **Key Trap:** Stative verbs (*know, love, have for possession, believe*) **cannot** be continuous:
  - ❌ *I have been knowing him for ten years.*
  - ✅ *I **have known** him for ten years.*

## 3. Narrative Tenses: Past Perfect Simple & Continuous

When narrating in the past, use the Past Perfect to step back into the **"Past before the Past"**:

| Tense | Form | Role in Narrative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Past Simple** | *V2 (walked, arrived)* | Main chronological sequence of events |
| **Past Continuous** | *was/were + V-ing* | Ongoing background activity when an event occurred |
| **Past Perfect Simple** | *had + V3* | Completed event that took place **before** the main narrative event |
| **Past Perfect Continuous**| *had been + V-ing* | Duration of an activity ongoing **up to** a specific past moment |

### Narrative Example
> *"When Sarah finally **arrived** (Past Simple) at the airport, she **realized** (Past Simple) that she **had forgotten** (Past Perfect Simple - happened earlier) her passport at home. She **had been packing** (Past Perfect Continuous - continuous activity before arriving) in such a rush that she left it on the kitchen counter."*

## 4. Advanced Future: Future Continuous & Future Perfect

Master these two structures for IELTS Speaking Part 3 and Writing Task 2 predictions:

### A. Future Continuous (`will be + V-ing`)
1. **Action in progress at a specific future moment:**
   *This time next week, I **will be lying** on the beach in Sihanoukville.*
2. **Polite inquiry regarding plans without pressuring:**
   ***Will you be using** the car tonight, or can I borrow it?*

### B. Future Perfect (`will have + V3`)
- **Action completed BEFORE a specified future deadline (with *by / by the time*):**
  - *By December 2026, the government **will have completed** the new airport expressway.*
  - *By the time you wake up tomorrow, I **will have already departed**.*

## 5. Modals of Deduction: Present & Past Speculation

Expressing certainty and degrees of probability:

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │          MODAL CERTAINTY            │
                  ├─────────────────────────────────────┤
 100% Sure YES    │ must + V1       | must have + V3    │
 50% Possible     │ may / might /   | might / may /     │
                  │ could + V1      | could have + V3   │
 100% Sure NO     │ can't + V1      | can't have + V3   │
                  └─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Examples
- **Present:** *The lights are off. They **must be** asleep. They **can't be** awake.*
- **Past:** *David didn't answer his phone. He **might have been** driving, or he **must have left** it in his office.*
- ❌ **Common Error:** Never use *must not have* for impossibility!
  - ❌ *He must not have been at the party; I didn't see him.*
  - ✅ *He **can't have been** at the party; I didn't see him.*

## 6. Third & Mixed Conditionals

### A. Third Conditional (100% Past Imaginary)
$$\mathbf{If} + \text{had} + \mathbf{V3}, \quad \text{would / could / might have} + \mathbf{V3}$$
- *If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed** the examination.*  
  *(Reality: I didn't study, so I didn't pass).*

### B. Mixed Conditionals (Past Cause $\leftrightarrow$ Present Result)
Combine past and present realities:

$$\text{Past Unreal Condition} \longrightarrow \text{Present Result}$$
$$\mathbf{If} + \text{had} + \mathbf{V3}, \quad \text{would} + \mathbf{V1}$$
- *If I **had won** the scholarship last year, I **would be studying** in London right now.*  
  *(Past cause: didn't win; Present result: not studying in London now).*

$$\text{Permanent / Present Condition} \longrightarrow \text{Past Result}$$
$$\mathbf{If} + \text{Past Simple (were/was)}, \quad \text{would have} + \mathbf{V3}$$
- *If she **were not so terrified** of heights, she **would have climbed** the mountain yesterday.*

## 7. Wishes, Regrets & Unreal Past (wish / if only)

The verb following *wish* or *if only* is always pushed **one step back into the past**:

| Regret Category | Target Time | Formula | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Wish** | Present | *wish + Past Simple* | *I wish I **lived** closer to my workplace.* (I live far away now). |
| **Past Regret** | Past | *wish + Past Perfect* | *I wish I **hadn't spent** so much money yesterday.* |
| **Annoyance / Request**| Future / Habit | *wish + would + V1* | *I wish you **would stop** making that irritating noise!* |

> 💡 **Notice:** *If only* carries the exact same grammatical rules as *wish*, but expresses **greater emotional intensity**:  
> *If only I **had listened** to my parents' advice!*

## 8. Impersonal & Reporting Passives

Used in formal journalism, academic papers, and IELTS essays to report claims without taking personal responsibility:

### Pattern 1: It + Passive Reporting Verb + that-clause
- *It **is reported that** inflation will decline next quarter.*
- *It **is believed that** the ancient temple dates back to the 9th century.*

### Pattern 2: Subject + Passive Reporting Verb + to-infinitive
$$\text{Subject} + \mathbf{is/are/was/were\ [said / believed / thought / reported]} + \mathbf{to + Infinitive\ (or\ to\ have\ V3)}$$
- *The CEO **is expected to resign** tomorrow.* (Future/Present)
- *The suspect **is believed to have fled** the country.* (Past action)

## 9. Causatives: Have & Get Something Done

When you arrange for another person (usually a professional) to perform a service for you:

$$\text{Subject} + \mathbf{have / get} + \text{Object (thing)} + \mathbf{Past\ Participle\ (V3)}$$

- *I **had my car serviced** yesterday.* (A mechanic serviced it).
- *She is going to **get her hair styled** for the wedding.*
- *He **had his wallet stolen** on the crowded bus.* (Unpleasant involuntary experience).

## 10. Non-Defining Relative Clauses & Formal Prepositions

### Non-Defining Relative Clauses (Extra Information)
- Always surrounded by **commas**.
- **Never** use *that*; use *which, who, where, whose, whom*.
- *Dr. Miller, **who** conducted the clinical research, will speak at the conference.*

### Formal Preposition Inversion (*Pied-Piping*)
In formal English, place the preposition **before** the relative pronoun (*whom/which*):
- Informal: *The project which we worked **on** won the award.*
- Formal B2: *The project **on which** we worked won the award.*
- Formal B2: *The professor **to whom** I submitted the paper was impressed.*

## 11. Concession & Contrast: Despite, In Spite Of, Whereas

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DESPITE / IN SPITE OF                │ ALTHOUGH / EVEN THOUGH / WHILE      │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Followed by: NOUN or GERUND (-ing)   │ Followed by: FULL CLAUSE (S + V)    │
│ *Despite the heavy rain, we went.*   │ *Although it rained heavily, we...* │
│ *In spite of being tired, he...*     │ *Even though he was tired, he...*   │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 💡 **Hack:** If you want to use a full clause after *despite*, add **the fact that**:  
> *Despite **the fact that it rained heavily**, we went out.*

## 12. Gerunds vs Infinitives with Radical Meaning Shifts

These 5 verbs change meaning completely depending on whether they take `-ing` or `to + V1`:

| Verb | Followed by Gerund (-ing) | Followed by to + Infinitive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **remember** | Memory of past action:  <br>*I **remember locking** the door.* | Obligation to perform action:  <br>*Please **remember to lock** the door.* |
| **forget** | Past experience forgotten:  <br>*I'll never **forget meeting** the president.*| Failed to execute task:  <br>*I **forgot to turn off** the oven.* |
| **stop** | Terminate an ongoing activity:  <br>*He **stopped smoking**.* | Pause in order to do another action:  <br>*He **stopped to smoke** a cigarette.* |
| **try** | Experiment with a novel method:  <br>*Why don't you **try restarting** your PC?*| Make an effort to do something arduous:  <br>*I **tried to lift** the heavy stone.* |
| **regret** | Sorrow for an earlier past action:  <br>*I **regret saying** those harsh words.* | Formal announcement of bad news:  <br>*We **regret to inform** you that...* |

## 13. Used To vs Be Used To vs Get Used To

| Expression | Structure | Meaning | Khmer Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Used to** | *used to + V1* | Past habit/state that is no longer true | ធ្លាប់ធ្វើអ្វីមួយកាលពីមុន តែឥឡូវលែងធ្វើហើយ |
| **Be used to** | *be used to + Noun / V-ing*| Accustomed to; familiar with; not strange | ទម្លាប់នឹង... (ស៊ាំហើយ មិនចម្លែកទេ) |
| **Get used to**| *get used to + Noun / V-ing*| The progressive process of becoming accustomed | កំពុងស៊ាំ/ចាប់ផ្តើមទម្លាប់នឹង... |

- *I **used to live** in a quiet village.* (Past state).
- *Living in Phnom Penh was hard at first, but now I **am used to the traffic**.*
- *It took me three months to **get used to working** the night shift.*

## 14. Mastering Cambridge B2 Key Word Transformations

In FCE Use of English Part 4, transform a sentence using a given word in **2 to 5 words**:

**Original:** *It is impossible that Mark stole the money because he was in Paris.*  
**Key Word:** **HAVE**  
**Solution:** *Mark **cannot have stolen** the money because he was in Paris.*

**Original:** *I am sorry that I didn't listen to your financial advice.*  
**Key Word:** **WISH**  
**Solution:** *I **wish I had listened to** your financial advice.*
