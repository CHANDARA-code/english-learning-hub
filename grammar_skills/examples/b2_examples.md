## Contextual Grammar Passage: The Urban Rewilding Initiative

**Passage Word Count:** 308 words  
**Grammar Focus:** Mixed Conditionals, Past Modal Deduction, Reporting Passives, Future Perfect, Causatives  

> **¶1** For over two decades, the municipal council of Greenford **had been struggling** to manage a desolate industrial wasteland along its southern perimeter. The site, **on which** chemical factories had previously operated, **was widely believed to have caused** severe soil contamination. However, rather than abandoning the area, environmental planners **had the entire parcel decontaminated** through advanced botanical remediation. If local authorities **had not intervened** with such boldness, the surrounding district **would not be flourishing** today as an ecological park.
>
> **¶2** Biologists **have been monitoring** the biodiversity index since native saplings **were introduced** in 2021. Yesterday, researchers announced that several species of migratory songbirds **had returned** to nest in the marshlands. Dr. Elaine Carter remarked that the wildlife **must have found** the newly restored wetlands hospitable, as such species **can't have survived** in the toxic environment that existed prior to rehabilitation. 
>
> **¶3** **Despite facing** initial skepticism from business syndicates, **who argued that** the land **should have been designated** for commercial office complexes, community support **has grown** exponentially. Urban residents, many of whom **used to suffer** from respiratory ailments, now enjoy pristine walking trails and clean air. The council **is reported to be drafting** legislation to protect an additional four hundred hectares of adjoining riverbanks.
>
> **¶4** By the time the next municipal election **takes place** in November 2028, engineers **will have completed** an integrated solar tramway connecting the reserve to the central railway terminal. Throughout the coming summer, local volunteers **will be planting** over fifty thousand native wildflowers along the pedestrian promenades. Planners emphasize that while ecological restoration is costly, sustainable civic transformation **can only be achieved** when natural ecosystems are prioritized over short-term speculative development.

## Grammar Analysis & Structural Highlights

| Sentence Extract from Text | Target Grammar Feature | Grammatical Nuance & Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *"...had been struggling to manage..."* | **Past Perfect Continuous** | Action ongoing up to a specific narrative past turning point |
| *"...site, on which chemical factories..."* | **Formal Relative Clause** | Preposition placed before relative pronoun (*pied-piping*) |
| *"...was widely believed to have caused..."*| **Impersonal Reporting Passive** | Passive reporting verb + Perfect Infinitive (*to have V3*) |
| *"...had the entire parcel decontaminated..."*| **Causative Passive** | Arranging for specialists to perform a service (*have + obj + V3*) |
| *"...If local authorities had not intervened... would not be flourishing..."*| **Mixed Conditional (Type 3 + 2)** | Past counterfactual action causing a present ongoing outcome |
| *"...must have found... can't have survived..."*| **Past Modal Deduction** | High certainty deduction about completed past events |
| *"...will have completed an integrated..."* | **Future Perfect Simple** | Event completed before a specific future deadline (*by November*) |
| *"...will be planting over fifty thousand..."*| **Future Continuous** | Activity in progress during a future timeframe (*throughout summer*) |

1. By the end of this decade, marine biologists ________ the entire coral reef mapping project.  
   A. will complete  
   B. will be completed  
   C. will have completed  
   D. have been completed  

2. Marcus looked exhausted yesterday because he ________ on his research paper all night.  
   A. has worked  
   B. had been working  
   C. is working  
   D. worked  

3. The ancient manuscript ________ by monks during the fourteenth century.  
   A. is believed to have written  
   B. is believed to have been written  
   C. believes to be written  
   D. was believing to write  

4. ________ the torrential rain and gale-force winds, the marathon participants completed the race.  
   A. Although  
   B. In spite  
   C. Despite  
   D. Even though  

5. I can't find my car keys anywhere; I ________ them inside the locked office.  
   A. must have left  
   B. must leave  
   C. should leave  
   D. can't have left

## Answer Key with Comprehensive Grammatical Annotations

### Part 1 Solutions
1. **Answer: C (will have completed).** The time marker *By the end of this decade* specifies a future completion deadline, requiring Future Perfect (*will have + V3*).
2. **Answer: B (had been working).** Past Perfect Continuous explains the past physical cause of exhaustion ongoing prior to *yesterday*.
3. **Answer: B (is believed to have been written).** Passive reporting structure with a past action: Subject + *is believed* + Perfect Passive Infinitive (*to have been + V3*).
4. **Answer: C (Despite).** Followed directly by a noun phrase (*the torrential rain and gale-force winds*). *Although* requires a full clause; *In spite* lacks *of*.
5. **Answer: A (must have left).** High certainty past deduction (*I am sure I left them* = *must have left*).

### Part 2 Solutions
6. **Answer: of** (Quantifier + preposition + relative pronoun: *many of which*).
7. **Answer: would** (Mixed conditional result clause indicating present state: *would still be causing*).
8. **Answer: been** (Present Perfect Continuous: *have been lobbying*).
9. **Answer: converted / transformed** (Causative structure: *had [something] + V3*).
10. **Answer: be / become** (Future state with *will be/become accustomed to*).

### Part 3 Solutions
11. **should not have rejected / shouldn't have rejected** (Past modal expressing criticism of an action: *shouldn't have + V3*).
12. **cannot have forged / can't have forged** (Past deduction of impossibility: *cannot have + V3*).
13. **are having their apartment renovated** (Present continuous causative: *are having + obj + V3*).
14. **would be attending** (Mixed conditional: Past unreal condition + Present continuous result: *would be attending*).
15. **spite of having an / spite of her** (*In spite of + noun / pronoun + noun*).

### Part 4 Solutions
16. **Correction: to have announced** (The action occurred *yesterday afternoon*; an impersonal reporting passive referring to past time requires the perfect infinitive *to have announced*).
17. **Correction: will have already started** (With *by the time [present clause]*, the main clause must be Future Perfect: *will have already started*).
18. **Correction: Despite the fact that it was / Although it was** (*Despite* cannot be followed immediately by a standard subject-verb clause without *the fact that*).
19. **Correction: If I had known** (Never use *would have* in the *if-clause* of a conditional; use Past Perfect *had known*).
20. **Correction: waking up** (In *be used to [something]*, *to* is a preposition and must be followed by a gerund: *am not used to waking up*).
