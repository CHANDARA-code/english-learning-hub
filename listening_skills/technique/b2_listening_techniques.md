> **Goal at B2:** Understand extended speech, academic lectures, radio documentaries, and complex multi-speaker discussions. Follow complex lines of argument, identify underlying attitudes, and decode fast, natural connected speech with regional accents.

> **Target Speech Rate:** 150–190 words per minute (wpm) featuring natural rhythm and colloquial elisions.

## 2. Advanced Connected Speech: Elision, Assimilation & Intrusion

At B2, you cannot decode speech sound-by-sound. You must understand how sounds morph in real-time:

### A. Elision (Disappearing Sounds)
Native speakers drop /t/ and /d/ when trapped between two other consonants:
- *last night* $\rightarrow$ **/lɑːs naɪt/** (The /t/ vanishes)
- *next day* $\rightarrow$ **/neks deɪ/**
- *hold tight* $\rightarrow$ **/həʊl taɪt/**
- *stand there* $\rightarrow$ **/stæn ðeə/**

### B. Assimilation (Morphed Sounds)
The ending consonant transforms to match the mouth position of the following consonant:
- **/t/ before /p, b, m/ becomes /p/**: *that boy* $\rightarrow$ **/ðæp bɔɪ/**, *credit card* $\rightarrow$ /ˈkredɪk kɑːd/
- **/d/ before /k, g/ becomes /ɡ/**: *good girl* $\rightarrow$ **/ɡʊɡ ɡɜːl/**
- **/n/ before /p, b, m/ becomes /m/**: *ten pens* $\rightarrow$ **/tem penz/**

### C. Intrusive Linking Sounds (/r/, /j/, /w/)
When two vowels meet across word boundaries, English speakers insert an unwritten bridge sound:
- **Intrusive /r/:** *law and order* $\rightarrow$ **/lɔː-r-ən ˈɔːdə/**, *media attention* $\rightarrow$ /ˈmiːdiə-r-əˈtenʃn/
- **Intrusive /j/ (y-sound):** *I agree* $\rightarrow$ **/aɪ-j-əˈɡriː/**, *she answered* $\rightarrow$ /ʃiː-j-ˈɑːnsəd/
- **Intrusive /w/:** *go out* $\rightarrow$ **/ɡəʊ-w-aʊt/**, *two others* $\rightarrow$ /tuː-w-ˈʌðəz/

## 3. Tonic Stress & Contrastive Intonation

In English, pitch changes highlight what is truly important. The most stressed word in a clause is the **Tonic Syllable**:

```
1. "I wanted to study BIOLOGY."       ──> Biology was the chosen subject.
2. "I WANTED to study biology."       ──> But circumstances prevented me from doing so!
3. "I wanted to study biology THERE." ──> The location was the crucial factor.
```

- When a speaker uses a **sudden fall-rise intonation (↘↗)**, they are signaling **doubt, reservation, or polite disagreement**:
  - *"Well, the methodology was sound, theoretically... (↘↗)"* $\rightarrow$ Implies: But in reality, it failed!

## 4. Navigating Multi-Speaker Academic Discussions (IELTS Section 3)

In academic discussions involving a university tutor and two students (e.g., *Jack and Chloe*), the exam questions test **who said what**:

```
Question: "What do BOTH students agree needs revision?"
            ├── Jack suggests revising the Introduction. (Chloe rejects ❌)
            ├── Chloe suggests redoing the survey data. (Jack rejects ❌)
            └── Both agree that the Conclusion is too brief. (Correct Answer ✅)
```

### Protocol for Multi-Speaker Tasks:
1. Identify the voices immediately: Voice A (Male student), Voice B (Female student), Voice C (Professor).
2. Listen for **collaboration signals:** *"I see what you mean, but...", "Exactly what I was thinking!", "Do you really think so?"*

## 5. Auditory Paraphrase Recognition

In B2 exams, you will **never** hear the words written in the question stem. You must match conceptual paraphrases:

| Written in Exam Paper | What the Audio Actually Says |
| :--- | :--- |
| *The facility is currently understaffed.* | *"They are operating with a skeleton crew at the moment."* |
| *The cost was higher than anticipated.* | *"It ended up exceeding our original budgetary estimates."* |
| *He abandoned his initial hypothesis.* | *"He decided to discard the theory he started out with."* |
| *The project was postponed indefinitely.* | *"They've put the whole venture on the back burner."* |

## 6. Decoding Speaker Stance, Skepticism & Hesitation

B2 questions regularly ask: *"How does the speaker feel about the proposal?"*

| Stance | Audio Clues & Discourse Markers |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Skeptical / Unconvinced** | *"That remains to be seen...", "I wouldn't hold my breath...", "It sounds fine on paper, but..."* |
| **Enthusiastic** | *"I couldn't agree more!", "It's an absolute game-changer!"* |
| **Indifferent / Resigned**| *"For what it's worth...", "At this stage, does it really matter?"* |
| **Ambivalent / Conflicted**| *"I'm torn between the two...", "There are merits on both sides, to be fair."* |

## 7. Tackling B2 / FCE / IELTS Listening Tasks

### 1. Sentence Completion (No more than TWO words)
- The answer is **always** an exact word from the recording (do not change verb tenses or noun forms).
- Watch out for singular vs plural: if the speaker says *"laboratories"*, writing *"laboratory"* will be marked incorrect.

### 2. Multiple Choice (3 or 4 options)
- **The Elimination Rule:** All options will usually be mentioned in the audio! Your job is to find the one that accurately matches the **condition** set in the question stem.

### 3. Multiple Matching (FCE Part 3)
- 5 speakers talking about a common theme.
- Focus on the **underlying emotion or turning point**, not specific nouns that appear as decoys across multiple options.

## 8. Auditory Stamina & Managing Information Clustered Bursts

In exams like IELTS Section 4 (academic lectures), speakers often speak for 40 seconds without answering any questions, and then deliver answers to **two questions within 10 seconds**:

```
[40-second background narrative...] ──> [Answer 34!] ──> (3 seconds) ──> [Answer 35!]
```

- **Defense:** Always read **two questions ahead** so that if the speaker moves quickly, you don't lose your place on the paper.

## 9. B2 Distractor Anatomy (The Half-Truth Trap)

Distractors in B2 listening exams follow distinct psychological tricks:

1. **The Third-Party Attribution Distractor:** The speaker quotes someone else's opinion (*"My supervisor suggested conducting interviews..."*), but then states their own preference (*"...but I opted for online surveys instead"*).
2. **The Temporal Inversion Distractor:** The speaker discusses past habits (*"We used to rely on diesel generators..."*) to trap candidates listening for current practices (*"...whereas nowadays solar arrays provide 90% of our power"*).

## 10. B2 Listening Self-Check List

- [ ] Can I recognize dropped /t/ and /d/ sounds in fast speech (*last night* = /lɑːs naɪt/)?
- [ ] Do I detect intrusive linking sounds (/r/, /j/, /w/) without getting confused?
- [ ] Can I distinguish between an individual speaker's view and a consensus agreed by all parties?
- [ ] Do I keep track of two questions simultaneously during fast academic monologues?
- [ ] Can I spot auditory half-truth distractors based on temporal shifts (*used to* vs *now*)?
