# 🟡 B1 Listening Techniques — Intermediate
## បច្ចេកទេសស្តាប់ កម្រិត B1 (មធ្យម)

> **Goal at B1:** Understand straightforward factual information about common everyday or work-related topics. Identify both general messages and specific details, provided speech is clearly articulated in a generally familiar accent.
> **Target Exams:** Cambridge B1 Preliminary (PET Listening), IELTS Band 4.5–5.0 (Listening Sections 1 & 2), CEFR B1.
> **Target Speech Rate:** 120–150 words per minute (wpm) in clear standard English.

## 1. What B1 Listening Looks Like

At B1, learners transition from isolated word recognition to processing continuous sentences in social, travel, and vocational settings.

| Area | B1 Benchmark | ន័យខ្មែរ (គោលដៅ) |

## 2. Acoustic Decoding: Connected Speech & Linking

Native speakers do not speak word-by-word. Words run together through **Catenation (Consonant-to-Vowel Linking)**:

```
Written Text:        "Pick it up and put it on the table."
What You Hear:       /pɪ-kɪ-tʌ-pən-pʊ-tɪ-tɒn ðə ˈteɪbl/
                      └── Pick-it-up-and-put-it-on...
```

### Key Linking Rules to Train Your Ears:
1. **Consonant + Vowel:** The ending consonant sound attaches to the beginning vowel of the next word:
   - *an apple* $\rightarrow$ /ə-ˈnæpl/
   - *first of all* $\rightarrow$ /fɜː-stə-vɔːl/
   - *hold on* $\rightarrow$ /həʊl-dɒn/
2. **Geminate Consonants:** When two identical consonant sounds meet, do not pronounce both; elongate the sound:
   - *black cat* $\rightarrow$ /blæˈkæt/ (NOT: *black-uh-cat*)
   - *bad day* $\rightarrow$ /bæˈdeɪ/

## 3. Weak Forms & The Schwa (/ə/)

Grammar/function words (*to, for, of, can, was, at, from, do*) lose their full vowel sound and reduce to the neutral **Schwa (/ə/)**:

| Function Word | Full Strong Form | Weak Form in Speech | Example in Spoken English |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **can** | /kæn/ | **/kən/** | *I can come tomorrow. $\rightarrow$ /aɪ kən kʌm.../* |
| **to** | /tuː/ | **/tə/** | *Going to school. $\rightarrow$ /ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə skuːl/* |
| **for** | /fɔː/ | **/fə/** | *This is for you. $\rightarrow$ /ðɪs ɪz fə juː/* |
| **was** | /wɒz/ | **/wəz/** | *He was waiting. $\rightarrow$ /hiː wəz ˈweɪtɪŋ/* |
| **of** | /ɒv/ | **/əv/** or **/ə/** | *Cup of tea. $\rightarrow$ /kʌp ə tiː/* |

> ⚠️ **Exam Insight:** If you listen for the strong form */kæn/*, you will miss *can*. The weak form is so quiet that intermediate listeners often think the speaker never said the word!

## 4. Mastering Numbers, Dates, Times & Spelling

Cambridge PET and IELTS Section 1 test basic data capture. Train these critical auditory distinctions:

### A. The "-teen" vs "-ty" Pitch Distinction
- **13–19 (-teen):** High pitch and stress on the **second syllable** $\rightarrow$ *thir-**TEEN*** /θɜːˈtiːn/, *four-**TEEN*** /fɔːˈtiːn/.
- **30–90 (-ty):** High pitch and stress on the **first syllable**, dropping fast $\rightarrow$ ***THIR**-ty* /ˈθɜːti/, ***FOR**-ty* /ˈfɔːti/.

### B. Commonly Confused Letter Names in Audio Spelling
- **A /eɪ/ vs E /iː/ vs I /aɪ/**: Remember *A* rhymes with *say*, *E* rhymes with *see*, *I* rhymes with *sky*.
- **G /dʒiː/ vs J /dʒeɪ/**: *G* ends in /iː/, *J* ends in /eɪ/.
- **B /biː/ vs P /piː/**: *B* is voiced; *P* has an explosive burst of air.
- **Double letters:** Audio often says *"double L"* or *"O-double-T-E-R"*.

### C. Telephone Numbers & Addresses
- Digit `0` is pronounced as **"Oh"** /əʊ/ or **"Zero"**.
- Postal codes combine numbers and letters: *SW1A 1AA* $\rightarrow$ *"S-W-one-A, one-double-A"*.

## 5. Predictive Listening: Pre-Reading Questions

Never listen passively. Use the 30–45 seconds before the audio plays to **predict**:

```
Question Stem: "The workshop begins at ________ on Saturday."
                  └── Prediction: Must be a TIME (e.g., 9:30 AM, 10:00).

Question Stem: "Contact person: Mrs. ________"
                  └── Prediction: A SURNAME (listen for spelling).

Question Stem: "Cost for students: £ ________"
                  └── Prediction: A PRICE or NUMBER.
```

1. **Underline keywords** that indicate *where* the speaker is in the audio.
2. **Guess the word class:** Is the missing word a Noun, a Number, an Adjective, or a Name?

## 6. Signposting Words in Spoken Guides & Monologues

Speakers use road-sign words to announce transitions. Tune your ears to them:

| Signpost Purpose | Spoken Clues to Listen For | What It Tells the Listener |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Introducing Topic** | *Today I'm going to talk about... / I'd like to begin by...* | Audio is starting Question 1 |
| **Sequence** | *First of all, then, next, after that, moving on to...* | Speaker is shifting to the next item on your worksheet |
| **Contrast / Correction**| *Actually, in fact, however, on the other hand...* | **Warning:** The previous detail was a trap! |
| **Conclusion** | *To wrap up, finally, all in all...* | Reaching the final exam question |

## 7. The Self-Correction & Distractor Trap

Test writers deliberately include a false answer right before or after the true answer:

```
Speaker: "We initially planned to hold the meeting in Room 12..."  (Distractor ❌)
Speaker: "...but because of the leak, it has been moved to Room 18." (Correct Answer ✅)
```

### Typical Self-Correction Signals:
- *"No, sorry, I meant..."*
- *"Actually, looking at my calendar..."*
- *"We used to, but now we..."*
- *"I had hoped to, but unfortunately..."*

> 💡 **Golden Rule:** The first number or date mentioned is frequently a **distractor**. Always listen through the entire sentence before writing your answer!

## 8. Tackling B1 Listening Question Formats

### 1. Form / Note / Table Completion
- Observe the **Word Limit** strictly (*"NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER"*).
- Check spelling: if you write *Febuary* instead of *February*, you lose the point!

### 2. Multiple Choice (Audio Extracts)
- Read all options before the audio begins.
- Listen for **synonyms**, not exact word matches. If Option A says *"He was pleased"*, the speaker will likely say *"He was delighted"* or *"He found it very enjoyable"*.

### 3. Matching Questions
- Keep your eyes on the entire list of options while listening.
- Cross out options as they are used so your choices narrow down.

## 9. The 4-Step B1 Listening Routine

```
[Phase 1: Pre-Listening]  ──> Read question stem, predict word type (Time, Name, Price)
        ↓
[Phase 2: First Audio]    ──> Focus on general gist and write initial pencil answers
        ↓
[Phase 3: Second Audio]   ──> Verify answers, check for self-corrections and traps
        ↓
[Phase 4: Post-Listening] ──> Check singular/plural, spelling, capital letters, word limits
```

## 10. B1 Listening Self-Check List

- [ ] Can I distinguish between *thirteen* and *thirty* in fast natural speech?
- [ ] Do I recognize weak forms of *can, to, for, was* (/kən, tə, fə, wəz/)?
- [ ] Do I wait for the speaker to finish before confirming an answer (checking for self-corrections)?
- [ ] Can I accurately transcribe English names spelled aloud letter-by-letter?
- [ ] Do I strictly adhere to word count restrictions (*"NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS"* )?
