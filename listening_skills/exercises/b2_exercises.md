### Exercise 4: Contrastive Tonic Stress & Implication
*Analyze where the speaker places tonic stress (bold caps) and choose the implied meaning:*

15. *"We **PLANNED** to launch the satellite in September."*  
    A. September was the only available launch window.  
    B. Circumstances changed, and the launch did not occur in September.  
    C. The rocket design was completely unviable.  

16. *"I didn't steal the **BLUE** sports car."*  
    A. Someone else stole the blue car.  
    B. I didn't steal any car whatsoever.  
    C. I stole a car, but it was a different color.  

17. *"The professor approved **MY** dissertation proposal."*  
    A. The professor rejected other students' proposals.  
    B. The professor approved the proposal reluctantly.  
    C. The student wrote someone else's proposal.

## Tier 2 — Multi-Speaker Academic Audio Tasks

### Audio Script: University Seminar on Renewable Energy Grids

> **[00:00] DR. GALLAGHER (Tutor, Scottish):** Alright, Liam and Chloe. Let’s focus on your joint seminar presentation on integrating microgrids into rural Scotland. Liam, how is the section on battery storage shaping up?
>
> **[00:11] LIAM (Student A, British):** Well, Dr. Gallagher, I was originally going to recommend lithium-ion storage arrays. But after reviewing recent life-cycle assessments, I realized that vanadium redox flow batteries are vastly superior for stationary grid resilience, despite their higher upfront installation fees.
>
> **[00:27] CHLOE (Student B, Canadian):** I completely agree with Liam on flow batteries. But my main concern is the public consultation phase. The local community council in Skye is vehemently opposed to overhead transmission lines because they claim it ruins panoramic tourism vistas.
>
> **[00:43] DR. GALLAGHER:** That’s a classic planning hurdle. What engineering alternative could address their aesthetic objections without bankrupting the project?
>
> **[00:51] CHLOE:** Underground cabling seemed like the obvious solution, but the geological survey revealed solid granite bedrock, making trenching financially prohibitive. So we're proposing routing the cables along the seabed around the bay instead.
>
> **[01:05] LIAM:** Exactly. Marine cabling avoids both the granite bedrock and the visual disruption.
>
> **[01:11] DR. GALLAGHER:** Excellent lateral thinking! Just make sure your final slide includes a cost-benefit matrix comparing seabed maintenance against traditional pylons.

## ✅ Answer Key with Auditory Annotations

### Tier 1 Solutions
1. **last night** (Elision of /t/ between /s/ and /n/).
2. **next day** (Elision of /t/ between /ks/ and /d/).
3. **hold tight** (Elision of /d/ between /l/ and /t/).
4. **used to believe** (Elision of /t/ in *used to*).
5. **basketball** (Elision of /t/ between /s/ and /b/).
6. **ten** (/n/ assimilates to bilabial /m/ before /p/).
7. **that** (/t/ assimilates to bilabial /p/ before /b/).
8. **good** (/d/ assimilates to velar /ɡ/ before /ɡ/).
9. **credit** (/t/ assimilates to velar /k/ before /k/).
10. **/r/** (Intrusive /r/ linking *media* and *attention*).
11. **/j/** (Intrusive /j/ linking *I* and *agree*).
12. **/w/** (Intrusive /w/ linking *go* and *out*).
13. **/r/** (Intrusive /r/ linking *law* and *and*).
14. **/w/** (Intrusive /w/ linking *two* and *others*).
15. **Answer: B** (Stressing *PLANNED* implies the plan was not fulfilled).
16. **Answer: C** (Stressing *BLUE* implies the speaker may have stolen a car of a different color).
17. **Answer: A** (Stressing *MY* contrasts with others' proposals).
18. **1 $\rightarrow$ C, 2 $\rightarrow$ D, 3 $\rightarrow$ A, 4 $\rightarrow$ B, 5 $\rightarrow$ E**
