## 1. From B1 to B2: The Lexical Transition

| Dimension | B1 Level | B2 Level | ន័យខ្មែរ (ការវិវឌ្ឍ) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Expression** | Direct & concrete (*a big problem*) | Abstract & qualified (*a pressing dilemma, a formidable obstacle*) | ប្រើពាក្យអរូបី និងមានទម្ងន់ច្បាស់លាស់ |
| **Collocations** | Everyday phrases (*make money*) | Academic partnerships (*allocate resources, generate revenue*) | ការផ្សំពាក្យបែបផ្លូវការ និងការងារ |
| **Word Formation** | Basic endings (*happy $\rightarrow$ happiness*) | Complex affixes (*predict $\rightarrow$ unpredictability*) | បម្លែងពាក្យស្មុគស្មាញដោយបន្ថែមបុព្វបទ/បច្ច័យ |
| **Phrasal Verbs** | Single literal meaning (*put on a coat*) | Polysemic abstract senses (*put on an exhibition, put forward an idea*) | យល់ន័យធៀបនៃកិរិយាសព្ទផ្សំ |

## 2. Advanced Word Formation: Latin/Greek Affixes

In Cambridge FCE Part 3 and academic writing, words undergo multiple transformations:

```
Root:                    PREDICT (Verb)
Add Suffix:              predictable (Adjective)
Add Negative Prefix:     UNpredictable (Opposite Adjective)
Add Noun Suffix:         unpredictabilITY (Abstract Noun)
```

### High-Impact B2 Prefixes:
- **over- / under-** (Degree): *overestimate, underestimate, overpopulated, underprivileged*
- **out-** (Exceed): *outweigh, outnumber, outperform, outlive*
- **mis-** (Incorrectly): *misinterpret, mislead, misunderstand, miscalculate*
- **inter-** (Between): *interconnected, interact, international, interdisciplinary*
- **co- / con-** (Together): *coexist, collaborate, co-author, cooperate*

### High-Impact B2 Suffixes:
- **-ify / -ize** (Verb forming): *simplify, clarify, justify, emphasize, prioritize*
- **-proof** (Resistance): *waterproof, soundproof, bulletproof, foolproof*
- **-worthy** (Deserving): *trustworthy, praiseworthy, noteworthy*

## 4. Polysemic Phrasal Verbs: Concrete to Abstract Shifts

At B2, common phrasal verbs acquire figurative, secondary meanings:

| Phrasal Verb | B1 Concrete Meaning | B2 Abstract / Professional Meaning | Exemplary B2 Usage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **take on** | to fight or board | to accept new responsibility or employ staff | *The company is **taking on** fifty new data analysts.* |
| **bring about**| to carry nearby | to cause or trigger a major historical change | *Technological innovation **brought about** the industrial revolution.* |
| **turn out** | to switch off a light| to develop in a particular way or have a result | *The experiment **turned out** to be a colossal success.* |
| **put forward**| to move physically | to propose a hypothesis or theoretical model | *The committee **put forward** three viable solutions.* |
| **come across** | to meet by chance | to make a particular impression on an audience | *She **comes across** as confident and highly competent.* |

## 5. Connotation vs Denotation: Nuanced Word Choice

Words sharing the same literal dictionary definition (**Denotation**) often evoke completely different emotional evaluations (**Connotation**):

```
                   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                   │             EVALUATIVE SPECTRUM         │
                   ├────────────────────┬────────────────────┤
 Neutral Denotation│ Positive (+)       │ Pejorative / (-)   │
 ──────────────────┼────────────────────┼────────────────────┤
 Thin in stature   │ slim, slender      │ skinny, gaunt      │
 Inquisitive mind  │ curious, probing   │ nosy, prying       │
 Firm in opinion   │ resolute, steadfast│ stubborn, obstinate│
 Economical        │ thrifty, prudent   │ stingy, miserly    │
 Confident         │ self-assured       │ arrogant, boastful │
                   └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 💡 **Writing Tip:** In IELTS essays, describing a leader as *resolute* scores points for positive nuance; describing them as *obstinate* signals stubborn arrogance.

## 6. Idiomatic Discourse & Formulaic Sequences

Integrate these natural fixed phrases into speaking and discursive writing:

- **a double-edged sword** (មុខពីរ): a situation with both significant benefits and severe hazards.  
  *Urbanization is a double-edged sword for developing economies.*
- **to see the bigger picture** (មើលឃើញទិដ្ឋភាពទូលំទូលាយ): to understand the entire context rather than isolated details.
- **a foregone conclusion** (លទ្ធផលដឹងមុន): a result that is obvious even before it occurs.
- **touch and go** (គ្រោះថ្នាក់/មិនប្រាកដប្រជា): precarious or uncertain.  
  *His recovery after the cardiac surgery was touch and go for twenty-four hours.*
- **play a pivotal role** (ដើរតួនាទីយ៉ាងសំខាន់): to be central to the outcome of an event.

## 7. Register Calibration: Informal to Formal Transformations

| Informal / Spoken (B1) | B2 Formal Written Equivalent | Example Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *to get rid of* | **to eliminate / to eradicate** | *eliminate unnecessary expenditures* |
| *to ask for* | **to request / to solicit** | *solicit feedback from clients* |
| *to put up with* | **to tolerate / to endure** | *tolerate substandard housing* |
| *to make sure* | **to ensure / to ascertain** | *ascertain the validity of the data* |
| *lots of* | **a substantial number / volume of**| *a substantial volume of evidence* |
| *about* (approx.) | **approximately / roughly** | *approximately twenty percent* |

## 8. The Academic Word List (AWL) Core Clusters

Familiarize yourself with the 6 most common AWL clusters tested in IELTS Reading and Writing:

1. **Analysis & Logic:** *analyze, evaluate, synthesize, deduce, infer, criteria*
2. **Creation & Formation:** *constitute, establish, generate, initiate, formulate*
3. **Investigation & Search:** *assess, investigate, monitor, survey, scrutinize*
4. **Change & Development:** *fluctuate, evolve, shift, transform, transition*
5. **Impact & Consequence:** *impact, affect, induce, precipitate, generate*
6. **Support & Justification:** *corroborate, validate, substantiate, sustain*

## 9. Mastering Cambridge FCE Word Formation (Part 3)

### The 4-Step Strategy:
1. **Analyze Sentence Gap Anatomy:** Read the sentence to determine the required part of speech (Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb).
2. **Check Contextual Polarity (+ / -):** Does the surrounding sentence require a negative prefix (*un-, in-, dis-, mis-* )?
3. **Plural vs Singular Check:** If a noun is required, does the verb or determiner indicate plural form?
4. **Spelling Verification:** Did you drop the *e* (*argue $\rightarrow$ argument*) or change *y* to *i* (*rely $\rightarrow$ reliable*)?
