## 1. The B1 Lexical Blueprint

At B1, language shifts from memorizing single words to understanding **how words connect with other words**:

| Lexical Dimension | Beginner (A1/A2) | Intermediate (B1) | ន័យខ្មែរ (ការវិវឌ្ឍ) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Word Form** | Single base form (*decide*) | Full word family (*decision, decisive, decisively*) | ចេះបម្លែងទម្រង់ពាក្យ (នាម កិរិយា គុណនាម) |
| **Phrasal Verbs** | Literal motion (*go in, sit down*) | Idiomatic daily phrasal verbs (*give up, look forward to*) | ប្រើកិរិយាសព្ទផ្សំដែលនិយមប្រើរាល់ថ្ងៃ |
| **Collocations** | Direct translation (*make exercise*) | Natural native collocations (*do exercise, take a break*) | ប្រើពាក្យផ្គុំបានត្រឹមត្រូវតាមទម្លាប់ដើម |
| **Prepositions** | Basic spatial (*in, on, at*) | Dependent prepositions (*fond of, interested in, proud of*) | ប្រើធ្នាក់ភ្ជាប់ជាមួយគុណនាម និងកិរិយាសព្ទ |

## 2. Word Families & Morphological Derivations

Learning one root word allows you to unlock 4 to 5 related words through standard prefixes and suffixes:

```
                  ┌───────────────┐
                  │ DECIDE (Verb) │
                  └───────┬───────┘
          ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
          ▼               ▼               ▼
    DECISION (Noun)  DECISIVE (Adj)  DECISIVELY (Adv)
          │               │
          ▼               ▼
   INDECISION (Neg) INDECISIVE (Neg)
```

### Essential B1 Derivation Patterns:
- **Verb $\rightarrow$ Noun (Action/Result):**
  - *-tion / -sion:* *inform $\rightarrow$ information*, *discuss $\rightarrow$ discussion*
  - *-ment:* *develop $\rightarrow$ development*, *enjoy $\rightarrow$ enjoyment*
  - *-ance / -ence:* *appear $\rightarrow$ appearance*, *differ $\rightarrow$ difference*
- **Noun/Verb $\rightarrow$ Adjective:**
  - *-ful / -less:* *help $\rightarrow$ helpful / helpless*, *care $\rightarrow$ careful / careless*
  - *-able / -ible:* *comfort $\rightarrow$ comfortable*, *rely $\rightarrow$ reliable*
- **Adjective $\rightarrow$ Adverb:**
  - *-ly:* *quick $\rightarrow$ quickly*, *patient $\rightarrow$ patiently*

## 3. Essential B1 Phrasal Verbs (Separable vs Inseparable)

A phrasal verb = **Verb + Preposition/Particle**. Its meaning is usually different from the individual words:

### A. Separable Phrasal Verbs (Object can go in the middle or at the end)
- *turn on:* *Please turn on the light.* / *Please turn the light on.* / *Turn it on.* (Pronouns **must** go in the middle: NOT *Turn on it*).
- *give up:* *She gave up smoking.* / *She gave smoking up.* / *She gave it up.*
- *put off:* *Don't put off your homework.* (= postpone / ពន្យារពេល).
- *call off:* *They called off the match due to rain.* (= cancel / លុបចោល).

### B. Inseparable Phrasal Verbs (Object must follow the preposition)
- *look after:* *Can you look after my dog?* (= take care of / ថែរក្សា).
- *look for:* *I am looking for my keys.* (= search / ស្វែងរក).
- *run out of:* *We have run out of milk.* (= have no more left / អស់).
- *get along with:* *She gets along well with her colleagues.* (= have a good relationship / ចុះសម្រុងគ្នា).

## 4. High-Frequency Collocations: Do vs Make vs Take

Using the wrong verb with a noun is the most common B1 lexical error:

| Verb | Rule of Thumb | Common B1 Collocations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **DO** | Duties, tasks, jobs, daily routines, non-specific actions | *do homework, do the dishes, do business, do your best, do someone a favor, do exercise* |
| **MAKE** | Creating, constructing, producing a physical product or emotional effect | *make a mistake, make a decision, make a phone call, make dinner, make money, make friends* |
| **TAKE** | Grabbing, transporting, actions involving time, medical interventions | *take a photo, take a shower, take an exam, take a break, take medicine, take public transport* |
| **HAVE** | Possessions, meals, experiences, personal events | *have breakfast, have a conversation, have a rest, have fun, have a cold, have an argument* |

## 5. Adjective + Preposition Combinations

Never memorize an adjective in isolation; always learn its dependent preposition:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ADJECTIVE + PREPOSITION              │ EXAMPLE SENTENCE                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ interested IN                        │ She is interested in learning code. │
│ good / bad AT                        │ He is exceptionally good at maths.  │
│ afraid / frightened / scared OF      │ Many children are afraid of spiders.│
│ famous / well-known FOR              │ Siem Reap is famous for its temples.│
│ proud OF                             │ Parents are proud of their children.│
│ keen ON                              │ I am not very keen on horror films. │
│ different FROM                       │ His opinion is different from mine. │
│ pleased / satisfied WITH             │ She was very pleased with the exam. │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 6. Synonym Ladders: Upgrading Repetitive Words

Stop using the words *good, bad, big, small, very* in every sentence. Upgrade your lexical ladder:

```
Basic (A1/A2)        B1 Intermediate Upgrade       Example Context
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
good                 fantastic, beneficial, great  "A balanced diet is beneficial."
bad                  unpleasant, harmful, awful    "Pollution causes harmful effects."
big                  enormous, massive, huge       "They live in an enormous house."
small                tiny, minimal, minor          "There were only minor errors."
interesting          fascinating, engaging         "The lecture was fascinating."
tired                exhausted, drained            "After the run, I was exhausted."
happy                delighted, thrilled           "She was delighted with her gift."
```

## 7. Contextual Word Deduction

When encountering an unknown word in an article, analyze its sentence anatomy before reaching for a dictionary:

1. **Identify the Part of Speech:** Look at word placement and ending (*-tion = noun, -ly = adverb, -able = adjective*).
2. **Examine Semantic Neighbors:**
   - *Example:* "The farmer used an **inexpensive** irrigation pump; in other words, it cost him very little money." $\rightarrow$ *inexpensive* = cheap / low cost.
3. **Check Connotation (+ / -):** Is the tone positive, negative, or neutral?

## 8. Commonly Confused Word Pairs (False Friends)

| Word Pair | Distinction & Rule | Correct Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **borrow vs lend** | *Borrow* = take something temporarily (ខ្ចី)  <br>*Lend* = give something temporarily (ឱ្យខ្ចី) | *Can I **borrow** your pen?*  <br>*Yes, I can **lend** it to you.* |
| **say vs tell** | *Say* = state words (*say that*)  <br>*Tell* = requires a personal object (*tell someone*) | *He **said** that he was tired.*  <br>*He **told me** that he was tired.* |
| **remember vs remind**| *Remember* = have in memory (ចងចាំ)  <br>*Remind* = make someone else remember (រំលឹក) | *I **remember** her name.*  <br>*Please **remind me** to call her.* |
| **lose vs loose** | *Lose* (verb) = fail to find / misplace (បាត់បង់)  <br>*Loose* (adj) = not tight (រលុង) | *Don't **lose** your keys.*  <br>*This belt is too **loose**.* |

## 9. Long-Term Lexical Retention: The Spaced Repetition System

```
Day 1: Learn new word (e.g., "accomplish") ──> 100% immediate recall
Day 2: Review (1st repetition)             ──> Prevents immediate decay
Day 4: Review (2nd repetition)             ──> Solidifies memory trace
Day 7: Review (3rd repetition)             ──> Moves into active working memory
Day 14: Review (4th repetition)            ──> Permanent semantic storage
```

### The 3-Sentence Vocabulary Notebook Method
For every new word you record in your study book:
1. Write the **Word, Part of Speech & Khmer Translation**: *reluctant (adj) = ស្ទាក់ស្ទើរ*.
2. Write one **Collocation**: *reluctant to agree*.
3. Write one **Personalized Sentence**: *I was reluctant to leave my hometown.*
